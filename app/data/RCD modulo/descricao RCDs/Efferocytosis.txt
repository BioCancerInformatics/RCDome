## Efferocytosis: Clearance Pathway, Not a Cell-Death Execution Modality

Efferocytosis is the regulated recognition, engulfment, intracellular processing, and immunological handling of dying or dead cells by professional or non-professional phagocytes [1-5].

It is **not an autonomous regulated cell-death modality**.

In canonical efferocytosis, the target cell is generally already committed to death, most commonly through apoptosis, before the phagocyte begins engulfment [1-4].

Therefore, the fundamental sequence is:

death stimulus → target-cell death program → acquisition of a clearance-recognition state → recruitment and recognition by a phagocyte → engulfment → efferosome maturation → lysosomal degradation of the corpse → metabolic and immunological adaptation of the phagocyte.

The key distinction is:

**apoptosis or another RCD mechanism kills the target cell; efferocytosis removes and digests the resulting dying cell or corpse.**

Consequently, molecules such as caspases can generate the signals that make an apoptotic cell recognizable, but they belong primarily to the upstream death program.

Conversely, Rac1, MerTK, TIM4, LC3-associated phagocytosis machinery, Rab proteins, TRPM7, and V-ATPase regulate corpse uptake and processing but do not constitute a death-execution machinery for the original target.

The physiological purpose of efferocytosis is not simply waste removal.

Efficient efferocytosis prevents accumulation and secondary lysis of apoptotic corpses, limits release of intracellular danger signals, regulates macrophage metabolism, promotes resolution of inflammation, and contributes to tissue homeostasis and repair [1-5,56].

---

## Mechanistic Definition

The most useful mechanistic framework contains four consecutive stages:

**1. Find-me signaling**

The dying cell releases soluble signals that attract potential phagocytes.

**2. Eat-me signaling**

The dying cell changes its surface composition, particularly by exposing phosphatidylserine.

**3. Recognition and engulfment**

Phagocyte receptors recognize the target directly or through bridging molecules and activate Rac1-dependent cytoskeletal remodeling.

**4. Efferosome maturation and degradation**

The engulfed corpse enters a single-membrane compartment that undergoes Rab-, LC3-associated phagocytosis-, Ca²⁺-, V-ATPase-, and lysosome-dependent maturation.

Thus:

dying cell → find-me cues + eat-me signals → phagocyte recruitment → receptor engagement → Rac1-dependent phagocytic cup → efferosome → acidifying phagolysosome → corpse degradation.

These are **clearance criteria**, not criteria demonstrating how the target originally died.

---

## Upstream Cell Death Precedes Canonical Efferocytosis

In the best-characterized setting, apoptosis occurs first.

The apoptotic machinery causes characteristic changes including:

caspase activation → cytoskeletal remodeling → cell shrinkage → membrane blebbing → nuclear condensation/fragmentation → phosphatidylserine exposure → release of soluble recruitment signals.

Several of these changes subsequently facilitate efferocytosis.

However, they should not be mechanistically reassigned to the clearance program.

For example:

caspase-3 → ROCK1 cleavage → actomyosin contraction → apoptotic blebbing

belongs to apoptotic execution [6-8].

The resulting apoptotic bodies may then become substrates for efferocytosis.

Thus:

**apoptotic body formation ≠ efferocytosis.**

It is a target-cell phenotype that facilitates subsequent clearance.

---

## Generation of the Eat-Me Signal: Phosphatidylserine

Phosphatidylserine, or PS, represents the dominant canonical eat-me signal.

In viable cells, PS is largely restricted to the cytoplasmic leaflet of the plasma membrane.

This asymmetry is actively maintained by ATP-dependent phospholipid flippases [9-12].

During apoptosis, executioner caspases remodel this system.

A major sequence is:

caspase-3/7 activation → activation of XKR8-mediated phospholipid scrambling + inactivation of ATP11C-mediated inward phospholipid transport → stable PS exposure on the extracellular surface [9-13,53].

This converts the membrane from:

PS predominantly inside

to

PS prominently exposed outside.

Extracellular PS then becomes accessible to phagocytic receptors and bridging proteins.

Caspase-dependent activation of XKR8 enables rapid loss of membrane phospholipid asymmetry.

Thus:

apoptotic caspases → XKR8 activation → bidirectional phospholipid scrambling → PS externalization.

XKR8 therefore helps generate the clearance-competent surface of the dying cell.

It is not an efferocytosis death executor.

Its function occurs downstream of apoptotic commitment and upstream of phagocyte recognition.

---

## ATP11C

ATP11C normally contributes to active inward transport of PS, thereby maintaining its enrichment on the cytoplasmic leaflet [10-11].

During apoptosis, caspase-dependent ATP11C inactivation prevents restoration of normal membrane asymmetry.

The coordinated logic is therefore:

XKR8 activation → PS moved outward,

while

ATP11C inactivation → PS no longer efficiently returned inward.

Together:

caspases → XKR8 ON + ATP11C OFF → persistent extracellular PS.

This produces a stable eat-me signal.

---

## PS Exposure Is Not Equivalent to Cell Death

PS externalization is highly useful for identifying apoptotic cells but should not be considered independently diagnostic of irreversible cell death.

PS can also be exposed in non-apoptotic contexts.

Other scrambling mechanisms, including Ca²⁺-responsive systems such as TMEM16F, can promote PS exposure without reproducing the canonical XKR8-dependent apoptotic program [9,14].

Therefore:

Annexin V positivity ≠ proof of apoptosis,

and

PS exposure ≠ proof of efferocytosis.

For efferocytosis, PS exposure must actually be followed by receptor-dependent recognition and engulfment.

---

## Find-Me Signaling

Before physical contact, dying cells can release soluble chemoattractant signals that increase the probability of encountering phagocytes [15-18].

These are traditionally called **find-me signals**.

Important examples include:

ATP and UTP,

lysophosphatidylcholine,

sphingosine-1-phosphate,

and CX3CL1-associated signals.

These signals are partially context dependent and should not be presented as universally required in every tissue.

Among them, extracellular nucleotides have particularly strong causal evidence.

---

## Pannexin-1–ATP/UTP–P2Y2 Axis

During apoptosis, executioner caspase activity can activate pannexin-1 channels.

This permits release of intracellular nucleotides into the extracellular environment.

The canonical sequence is:

apoptosis → caspase-3/7 activity → pannexin-1 channel activation → ATP/UTP release → extracellular nucleotide gradient → P2Y2 receptor activation on responding phagocytes → migration toward the dying cell [16].

Therefore, ATP and UTP serve as directional recruitment signals.

They should not be confused with the eat-me signal.

Their main role is:

**find the target**, rather than **recognize the target surface for engulfment**.

---

## Lysophosphatidylcholine–G2A Signaling

Another context-dependent find-me pathway involves lysophosphatidylcholine.

Apoptotic signaling can engage phospholipase activity and generate extracellular lysophosphatidylcholine.

Monocytes and macrophages can respond through G2A-dependent signaling [17].

Thus:

apoptosis → phospholipase-associated LPC generation → LPC release → G2A-associated phagocyte migration.

This pathway contributes to recruitment but is not universally dominant in all efferocytic systems.

---

## Sphingosine-1-Phosphate

Sphingosine-1-phosphate can also function as an apoptosis-associated recruitment and macrophage-regulatory signal.

The conceptual sequence is:

dying cell → sphingosine kinase-associated S1P production/release → S1P receptor signaling on nearby cells → phagocyte recruitment and/or acquisition of a pro-efferocytic state.

S1P signaling can additionally influence macrophage transcriptional and metabolic responses after corpse recognition.

Again, S1P should be treated as one component of a context-dependent find-me network rather than a universal mandatory initiator.

---

## CX3CL1

Apoptotic cells can release CX3CL1-associated signals.

CX3CR1-expressing macrophages or microglia can respond to this signal.

CX3CL1 signaling can also promote expression of molecules that increase efferocytic competence, including MFG-E8 in selected settings.

Thus:

apoptotic cell → CX3CL1 → CX3CR1-positive phagocyte → recruitment/priming → enhanced corpse recognition.

---

## Recognition of Phosphatidylserine

After a phagocyte reaches the dying cell, exposed PS provides a major surface-recognition signal.

Recognition occurs through two broad strategies:

**direct recognition**

or

**bridging-molecule-dependent recognition** [19-22].

Direct PS-recognition systems include receptors such as:

TIM4,

BAI1,

stabilins,

selected CD300-family proteins,

and other PS-sensitive receptor systems.

Indirect recognition uses soluble bridging molecules.

Important examples include:

Gas6,

Protein S,

and MFG-E8.

These molecules bind PS on the dying cell and simultaneously engage receptors on the phagocyte.

---

## TIM4

TIM4 is a high-affinity PS-binding receptor expressed on selected macrophage populations.

Its principal role is often described as tethering or capturing PS-positive cells.

Thus:

PS-positive dying cell → TIM4 engagement → physical stabilization of corpse–phagocyte contact.

TIM4 alone does not necessarily provide the strongest intracellular engulfment signal.

TIM4 binds phosphatidylserine on apoptotic cells and can cooperate with other receptors, such as MerTK, to promote efficient internalization and clearance of apoptotic cells [19].

This illustrates an important principle:

**recognition and engulfment signaling can be divided among different receptors.**

---

## MerTK

MerTK is a member of the TAM receptor tyrosine kinase family.

Rather than directly recognizing PS, MerTK commonly engages PS through bridging molecules including Gas6 and Protein S [19-23].

The sequence is:

PS on dying cell → Gas6/Protein S binding → MerTK engagement on phagocyte → receptor tyrosine kinase signaling → cytoskeletal engulfment machinery.

MerTK therefore provides strong internalization signaling.

TIM4 and MerTK can cooperate:

TIM4 → target tethering,

MerTK → signaling toward engulfment.

Experimental evidence demonstrates that these receptors can participate in distinct yet complementary signaling programs [23,55].

---

## TAM Receptors

The TAM family contains:

TYRO3,

AXL,

and MerTK.

Their relative contributions depend on the phagocyte type, tissue environment, inflammatory state, and available bridging proteins [24].

For canonical macrophage efferocytosis, MerTK is especially prominent.

TAM-receptor signaling also has strong immunoregulatory effects and can promote anti-inflammatory or tissue-repair-associated cellular states.

This becomes particularly relevant in tumors, where the same mechanisms that normally support tissue homeostasis can contribute to immune suppression.

---

## MFG-E8 and Integrins

MFG-E8 provides another bridging system.

MFG-E8 binds phosphatidylserine on apoptotic cells and integrins on the phagocyte, particularly αv-family integrins.

Thus:

PS → MFG-E8 → αvβ3/αvβ5-associated recognition → Rac1-dependent engulfment.

Integrins therefore provide an alternative route connecting the same eat-me ligand to cytoskeletal uptake machinery.

MerTK and αvβ5 signaling can also cooperate [25].

---

## BAI1

BAI1 can directly recognize PS and couple recognition to intracellular machinery that activates Rac1-dependent engulfment.

The BAI1 pathway reinforces a general convergence principle:

multiple upstream recognition receptors → shared Rac1-dependent actin remodeling.

Thus, receptor diversity does not imply the existence of completely separate terminal engulfment mechanisms.

---

## Receptor Redundancy

A defining characteristic of efferocytosis is receptor redundancy.

A phagocyte can express multiple PS-recognition receptors simultaneously.

Different tissues use different combinations.

Consequently, genetic deletion or pharmacological inhibition of a single receptor often reduces efferocytosis without completely abolishing it [24].

Therefore, it is usually incorrect to describe:

PS → one universal efferocytosis receptor.

A more accurate model is:

PS + tissue-specific bridging molecules → combinatorial receptor network → convergent cytoskeletal signaling.

---

## CD47–SIRPα as a Don't-Eat-Me Checkpoint

Healthy cells actively resist inappropriate engulfment.

One of the best-characterized inhibitory systems is:

CD47 on the potential target → SIRPα on the phagocyte [26].

SIRPα contains inhibitory cytoplasmic motifs that become phosphorylated and recruit phosphatases such as SHP1 and SHP2.

The resulting signal suppresses engulfment.

Thus:

CD47 → SIRPα → SHP1/SHP2-associated inhibitory signaling → reduced phagocytic uptake.

The final decision therefore depends on a balance:

eat-me signals versus don't-eat-me signals.

Strong PS recognition combined with reduction of inhibitory signaling favors efferocytosis.

Persistent CD47–SIRPα signaling opposes it.

---

## Recognition Is Not Yet Engulfment

Binding of a phagocyte to a PS-positive cell does not automatically mean the cell has been internalized.

Recognition and internalization are experimentally separable steps.

This distinction is particularly clear for receptors such as TIM4, which can tether targets efficiently but require cooperation with signaling receptors for robust internalization.

Therefore, efferocytosis assays should distinguish:

target binding,

internalization,

efferosome formation,

and final acidification/degradation.

A simple count of macrophages associated with apoptotic cells does not necessarily demonstrate successful efferocytosis.

---

## Cytoskeletal Signaling Converges on Rac1

Despite the diversity of recognition receptors, a major common downstream event is activation of Rac1 [1,27-28].

Rac1 coordinates actin polymerization required for membrane extension around the dying cell.

Thus:

corpse recognition → intracellular signaling → Rac1 activation → actin rearrangement → phagocytic cup formation → corpse internalization.

Rac1 therefore represents one of the central execution molecules of the **engulfment process**.

However:

Rac1 executes phagocytosis,

not death of the target.

---

## TRIO–RhoG–ELMO–DOCK180 Pathway

One of the best-characterized signaling routes connecting recognition to Rac1 involves TRIO, RhoG, ELMO, and DOCK180 [1,27].

The sequence is:

receptor signaling → TRIO activation → RhoG-GTP → ELMO recruitment → ELMO–DOCK180 complex → Rac1 activation → actin polymerization.

DOCK180 functions as a Rac guanine nucleotide exchange factor when associated with ELMO.

The pathway therefore converts receptor occupancy into a localized actin-remodeling response.

The resulting polymerization pushes the phagocyte membrane around the apoptotic cell.

---

## ELMO–DOCK180–Rac1 Module

The ELMO–DOCK180 system is evolutionarily conserved and represents a central engulfment module [1].

Its functional output is:

GDP-bound Rac1 → GTP-loaded Rac1.

Activated Rac1 promotes actin remodeling at the phagocytic cup.

Thus:

ELMO/DOCK180 → Rac1-GTP → actin polymerization → membrane extension.

Loss of this pathway impairs apoptotic-cell engulfment without reversing the upstream death of the target.

This again illustrates the conceptual separation between killing and clearance.

---

## LRP1–GULP Pathway

A second engulfment-signaling route involves LRP1 and GULP.

LRP1-mediated recognition can activate a cytoskeletal program converging toward Rac1.

However, the biochemical steps connecting the LRP1–GULP system to Rac activation are less completely defined than the ELMO–DOCK180 pathway.

Therefore, it is most accurate to represent:

LRP1 → GULP → downstream cytoskeletal signaling → Rac1-associated engulfment,

with the intermediate molecular connection remaining incompletely resolved.

---

## MerTK–FAK–p130CAS–CrkII–DOCK180 Signaling

MerTK can also connect to Rac1 through a signaling network involving Src-family activity, focal adhesion kinase, p130CAS, CrkII, and DOCK180 [25].

A representative sequence is:

MerTK activation → Src/FAK signaling → p130CAS → CrkII → DOCK180 → Rac1 → actin remodeling.

This signaling can cooperate with αvβ5 integrins.

Thus, multiple receptor architectures converge on the same final engulfment machinery.

---

## Calcium During Engulfment

Calcium signaling contributes directly to internalization.

MerTK-dependent Ca²⁺ influx has been shown to regulate extension and closure of the phagocytic cup [29].

The pathway includes:

MerTK engagement → Ca²⁺ influx → calmodulin activation → MLCK regulation → myosin light-chain phosphorylation → coordinated F-actin remodeling → cup extension and closure.

Importantly, actin must not only polymerize.

Specific regions of F-actin also need to disassemble or reorganize as the membrane closes around the corpse.

Therefore, successful engulfment requires temporally coordinated polymerization and depolymerization rather than indiscriminate actin accumulation.

---

## Formation of the Phagocytic Cup

After Rac1 activation, the phagocyte plasma membrane progressively extends around the target.

The sequence is:

target attachment → localized Rac1 activation → actin polymerization → pseudopod extension → membrane enclosure of target → cup closure.

The resulting structure separates the corpse from the extracellular environment.

Once sealing is complete, the corpse resides within a single-membrane intracellular compartment commonly referred to as an efferosome or apoptotic-cell-containing phagosome.

This represents a major transition in the clearance process.

It is not the point at which the target originally dies.

---

## Efferosome Formation

After phagocytic-cup closure:

extracellular corpse → internalized efferosome.

The phagocyte must now convert this newly formed compartment into a degradative organelle.

This requires membrane trafficking, phosphoinositide remodeling, Rab GTPase transitions, ion transport, lysosomal fusion, and hydrolase activation [30-33].

Failure at this stage can result in incomplete corpse digestion despite successful uptake.

Thus, engulfment and digestion must also be treated as separable stages.

---

## VPS34 and Phosphatidylinositol-3-Phosphate

Class III PI3K/VPS34 contributes to phosphatidylinositol-3-phosphate generation on phagosomal membranes.

This lipid creates docking sites for proteins involved in vesicular trafficking and maturation.

A simplified sequence is:

new efferosome → VPS34 activation → PI3P generation → recruitment of maturation machinery.

This helps establish the identity of the early intracellular corpse-containing compartment.

---

## Rab5-to-Rab7 Transition

Phagosome maturation involves sequential Rab GTPase recruitment.

Rab5 is associated predominantly with early stages.

As maturation proceeds, the compartment acquires Rab7-associated late endosomal characteristics.

Thus:

new efferosome → Rab5-positive early stage → Rab conversion → Rab7-positive late stage → lysosomal fusion.

This transition progressively shifts the compartment from uptake toward degradation.

---

## LC3-Associated Phagocytosis

LC3-associated phagocytosis, or LAP, represents an important non-canonical autophagy-related pathway involved in processing engulfed corpses [30,34-35].

LAP should not be confused with canonical macroautophagy.

In canonical autophagy:

cytoplasmic material → double-membrane autophagosome → lysosomal degradation.

In LAP:

extracellular cargo is first engulfed into a **single-membrane phagosome**, after which LC3 is conjugated to that phagosomal membrane.

Thus:

efferocytosis → single-membrane efferosome → LAP machinery → LC3 decoration → enhanced maturation/degradation.

LAP therefore contributes to clearance rather than independently killing the apoptotic cell.

---

## Rubicon

Rubicon is an important component of LAP [34].

Within this pathway, Rubicon helps organize the non-canonical LC3-associated processing of the corpse-containing phagosome.

The general sequence is:

corpse engulfment → LAP signaling → Rubicon-associated PI3KC3 activity → LC3 conjugation to phagosomal membrane → accelerated maturation.

Loss of Rubicon can impair efficient processing of engulfed apoptotic material.

However, recognition and initial uptake may still occur.

Thus:

Rubicon deficiency → defective intracellular corpse processing,

not

rescue of the upstream dying target.

---

## ATG5 and ATG7

LAP uses components of the canonical autophagy conjugation machinery, including ATG5 and ATG7.

These proteins facilitate LC3 lipidation on the phagosomal membrane.

Thus:

LAP activation → ATG5/ATG7-dependent LC3 conjugation → LC3-positive efferosome → efficient maturation.

Because LAP shares machinery with autophagy, experimental interpretation requires care.

LC3 recruitment around an apoptotic corpse does not mean that the corpse died through autophagy-dependent cell death.

It means autophagy-related proteins are helping the phagocyte digest extracellular cargo.

---

## TRPM7-Dependent Periphagosomal Ca²⁺ Signaling

TRPM7 provides an additional regulatory layer after engulfment.

Periphagosomal Ca²⁺ signaling mediated by TRPM7 is required for efficient efferosome maturation and acidification [36].

Therefore:

sealed efferosome → TRPM7-associated Ca²⁺ signaling → maturation machinery → efficient acidification and cargo degradation.

This identifies ion flux as a mechanistically important component of the intracellular digestive phase.

TRPM7 is not a death receptor or target-cell executor.

Its function belongs entirely to the phagocyte after engulfment.

---

## V-ATPase and Acidification

Mature phagolysosomal degradation requires strong acidification.

Vacuolar ATPases transport protons across the phagosomal/lysosomal membrane.

Thus:

V-ATPase activity → H⁺ accumulation within efferosome/phagolysosome → low luminal pH → activation of acid-dependent degradative enzymes.

Different V-ATPase subunits can contribute to different maturation stages [37].

Acidification is therefore one of the clearest biochemical indicators that uptake has progressed into effective corpse processing.

---

## Lysosomal Fusion

Rab7-associated late efferosomes ultimately interact and fuse with lysosomal compartments.

This delivers lysosomal hydrolases to the corpse.

The sequence becomes:

Rab7-positive efferosome → lysosomal fusion → acidic phagolysosomal compartment → proteases, nucleases, lipases and other hydrolases degrade corpse macromolecules.

At this stage the cellular remains are physically destroyed.

Importantly, this is **corpse destruction after death**, not the original lethal execution event.

---

## Physical Destruction of the Corpse

Once contained inside the acidic phagolysosome:

proteins → lysosomal proteolysis,

DNA/RNA → nuclease-dependent degradation,

membranes/lipids → lipolytic processing,

other macromolecules → enzymatic catabolism.

The target's structural remnants are therefore dismantled inside the phagocyte.

This creates an important semantic distinction:

**death execution** = irreversible loss of target-cell viability.

**corpse execution/digestion** = physical destruction of remains after engulfment.

Only the first defines RCD.

---

## Metabolic Processing of Corpse-Derived Material

Engulfing an entire cell places a substantial metabolic burden on a phagocyte.

Efferocytes therefore reprogram their metabolism to process imported proteins, lipids, nucleotides, cholesterol, and other molecules [38-40].

Corpse-derived nutrients can also act as metabolic signals.

For example, metabolism of apoptotic-cell-derived arginine supports continued efferocytic capacity and inflammation resolution [39].

Apoptotic-cell-derived methionine can influence macrophage epigenetic regulation through DNMT3A-associated mechanisms [40].

Thus:

corpse digestion → liberation of metabolites → metabolic and epigenetic reprogramming of efferocyte → altered inflammatory/tissue-repair phenotype.

This demonstrates that efferocytosis is substantially more than physical phagocytosis.

---

## Continual Efferocytosis

Professional phagocytes often need to consume multiple apoptotic cells sequentially.

This process is termed continual efferocytosis.

Successful digestion and metabolic handling of one corpse can influence whether the macrophage remains capable of taking up additional targets.

Therefore:

first corpse → intracellular digestion/metabolic adaptation → restoration or enhancement of phagocytic capacity → subsequent corpse uptake.

Disruption of this adaptation can result in corpse accumulation even if initial recognition machinery remains intact.

---

## Anti-Inflammatory and Pro-Resolving Signaling

One of the defining biological consequences of canonical efferocytosis is suppression of excessive inflammatory signaling.

Recognition and processing of apoptotic cells can shift macrophages toward programs that favor:

resolution,

tissue repair,

clearance of additional corpses,

and limitation of inflammatory mediator production [3,5,41].

This is one major difference between efficient efferocytosis and uncontrolled release of intracellular material after necrotic rupture.

Therefore:

prompt apoptotic-cell clearance → intracellular containment of potentially inflammatory cargo → pro-resolving efferocyte response.

---

## Why Rapid Clearance Is Important

Apoptotic cells initially maintain substantial plasma-membrane integrity.

This provides a time window during which efferocytosis can remove them without widespread release of intracellular contents [42].

If uptake occurs promptly:

apoptosis → PS exposure → efferocytosis → intracellular lysosomal degradation.

If uptake fails:

apoptosis → persistent uncleared corpse → progressive membrane dysfunction → secondary necrosis → intracellular contents released.

Thus, efferocytosis determines the fate of the **corpse** and the inflammatory environment rather than the original mechanism of target-cell death.

---

## Secondary Necrosis

Secondary necrosis occurs when apoptotic cells remain uncleared for sufficiently long periods.

As the dying cell can no longer maintain membrane homeostasis:

membrane permeability increases → repair becomes inadequate → plasma-membrane integrity is eventually lost → intracellular molecules escape.

The sequence is:

apoptosis completed → failed/delayed efferocytosis → prolonged corpse persistence → secondary membrane failure → secondary necrosis.

Secondary necrosis therefore represents a failure of timely corpse disposal rather than part of canonical efferocytosis.

---

## Membrane Repair Can Delay Secondary Necrosis

Recent evidence indicates that dying cells retain limited mechanisms capable of delaying terminal membrane rupture.

ATG9A supports lysosomal repair and protects the plasma membrane from permeabilization, and autophagy extends sebocyte survival during differentiation [54].

Thus:

Ca²⁺ influx → ATG9A-associated membrane repair → delayed secondary rupture → prolonged opportunity for efferocytic clearance [57].

Disrupting this protective mechanism accelerates secondary necrosis.

This provides a direct mechanistic link between clearance timing and inflammatory outcome.

---

## DAMP Containment

Efficient efferocytosis limits uncontrolled release of damage-associated molecular patterns.

By contrast, secondary necrotic or primary lytic death can release proteins such as HMGB1 and other intracellular components capable of stimulating inflammatory responses [43-44].

Thus:

successful early efferocytosis → intracellular sequestration of corpse → controlled digestion → reduced extracellular DAMP burden.

Failed clearance → secondary rupture → DAMP release → inflammation.

This is one major physiological reason why tissues invest heavily in apoptotic-cell recognition.

---

## Efferocytosis of Necroptotic and Pyroptotic Cells

Efferocytosis is not restricted absolutely to apoptotic cells.

Cells undergoing necroptosis or pyroptosis can also expose PS or other recognizable ligands and can be engulfed under certain conditions [45].

However, the surrounding biological context differs substantially.

Apoptotic cells commonly remain membrane-contained during early clearance.

Necroptotic and pyroptotic cells can generate early membrane permeability, DAMP release, and inflammatory signals.

Therefore:

same phagocytic machinery ≠ same immunological consequence.

The clearance pathway can overlap, while the original RCD execution mechanisms remain distinct.

---

## Phosphatidylserine During Non-Apoptotic Death

PS exposure itself is not exclusively apoptotic.

Non-apoptotic dying cells can transiently or stably externalize PS.

Consequently:

PS-positive target + engulfment

does not prove that the upstream death mechanism was apoptosis.

The upstream RCD must be independently characterized.

This distinction is particularly important when studying necroptosis, pyroptosis, or tumor cells.

---

## Efferocytosis Can Be Pathologically Rewired

Although canonical efferocytosis is largely homeostatic, inflammatory environments can alter the response of the phagocyte.

A striking example occurs under strong TNF signaling, where efferocytosis can become coupled to caspase-8-dependent lytic pyroptotic signaling in the efferocyte [46].

This can be represented as:

inflammatory TNF environment + efferocytic signaling → caspase-8-dependent rewiring → pyroptotic/IL-1β-associated response of the phagocyte.

This does **not** mean that efferocytosis itself is pyroptosis.

Rather:

efferocytosis provides the phagocytic context,

while

a separate death pathway becomes activated in the efferocyte.

---

## Tumor Microenvironment

Efferocytosis has major context-dependent effects in cancer.

Tumors contain abundant dying cells because of hypoxia, nutrient limitation, immune attack, and therapy.

Macrophages that continually engulf apoptotic tumor cells can acquire immunoregulatory and tissue-remodeling phenotypes [24,47-48].

For example:

apoptotic tumor cells → MerTK/TAM-associated efferocytosis → macrophage reprogramming → increased immune tolerance and tissue-remodeling signals.

In some cancers this can facilitate tumor progression.

Therefore, a pathway that is strongly beneficial during wound resolution can become detrimental when exploited by malignant tissue.

---

## MerTK and Tumor Immune Suppression

MerTK-mediated uptake can promote macrophage phenotypes associated with immune tolerance.

In osteosarcoma, MerTK-mediated efferocytosis has been linked to M2-associated polarization and increased PD-L1 expression [47].

Thus:

tumor-cell apoptosis → PS-dependent MerTK signaling → efferocytosis → immunoregulatory macrophage phenotype.

This is a downstream consequence of corpse clearance.

MerTK does not execute tumor-cell apoptosis.

---

## Proangiogenic Reprogramming

Recent work also demonstrates that efferocytosis can alter chromatin and transcriptional states of tumor-associated macrophages and promote proangiogenic functions [48].

This reinforces a broader concept:

corpse uptake → metabolic substrate influx + receptor signaling + epigenetic remodeling → long-term functional reprogramming of the efferocyte.

Therefore, efferocytosis should be understood as an immunometabolic signaling event in addition to a clearance mechanism.

---

## Efferocytosis in Atherosclerosis

Atherosclerotic plaques provide another major physiological and pathological context.

Macrophage apoptosis is common in advanced plaques.

Efficient efferocytosis limits necrotic-core development, whereas defective clearance allows dead-cell accumulation and secondary necrosis [49].

The sequence is:

macrophage apoptosis → efficient efferocytosis → corpse removal → reduced necrotic-core expansion.

Versus:

macrophage apoptosis → defective efferocytosis → corpse accumulation → secondary necrosis → inflammatory plaque progression.

This illustrates why defects in clearance can profoundly influence disease even without altering the upstream frequency of cell death.

---

## Efferocytosis in Tissue Repair

Efferocytosis participates in repair after injury in organs including heart, brain and other tissues [50].

The mechanism is not simply disappearance of dead cells.

Efferocytes integrate:

receptor signaling,

corpse-derived metabolites,

cytokine regulation,

and tissue-remodeling pathways.

Thus:

injury → cell death → efferocytosis → inflammatory resolution + metabolic reprogramming + repair signaling.

The same architecture can produce different outcomes depending on the tissue environment.

---

## Morphological Sequence

For a canonical apoptotic target, the chronology is:

healthy cell with intracellular PS → apoptotic commitment → executioner-caspase activity → cell shrinkage and chromatin condensation → ROCK1/actomyosin-associated blebbing → XKR8 activation and ATP11C inactivation → PS exposure → find-me signal release → phagocyte recruitment → corpse recognition → phagocytic-cup formation → engulfment → efferosome → acidification/digestion.

Two outcomes then diverge.

### Efficient clearance

apoptotic cell → prompt engulfment → intracellular corpse digestion → minimal secondary leakage.

### Failed clearance

apoptotic cell → prolonged extracellular persistence → progressive membrane permeability → secondary necrosis → DAMP release.

---

## What Is the Point of No Return?

There is no meaningful universal **efferocytosis point of no return for cell death**, because the target is generally already irreversibly committed through an upstream death pathway before efferocytosis starts.

For an apoptotic target, the relevant death commitment might be:

widespread MOMP,

or

sufficiently sustained executioner-caspase activity,

depending on the apoptotic pathway.

These events belong to apoptosis.

By contrast:

XKR8-dependent PS exposure marks acquisition of a strong clearance signal.

It is more accurately interpreted as:

**commitment to recognition/removal**

rather than

**commitment to death**.

Likewise, phagocytic-cup closure marks successful internalization of the target, but this is an engulfment checkpoint, not a death checkpoint.

---

## What Physically Kills the Target?

In canonical apoptotic-cell efferocytosis, the target is killed by its apoptotic machinery.

For example:

intrinsic apoptosis → BAX/BAK → MOMP → apoptosome → caspase-9 → caspase-3/7 → cellular demolition.

Or:

extrinsic apoptosis → death receptor → caspase-8 → caspase-3/7 ± mitochondrial amplification → death.

Only afterward:

caspase-mediated PS exposure/find-me signals → phagocyte recognition → efferocytosis.

Thus, it would be mechanistically incorrect to state:

MerTK kills the apoptotic cell,

TIM4 kills the apoptotic cell,

Rac1 kills the apoptotic cell,

or

lysosomal degradation is the original cause of death.

The lysosome destroys the **already dying/dead target after engulfment**.

---

## What Physically Destroys the Corpse?

Once internalized, the answer changes.

Corpse destruction is driven by:

efferosome maturation → acidification → lysosomal fusion → lysosomal hydrolases → degradation of corpse proteins, nucleic acids, lipids and organelles.

Therefore:

apoptosis executes viability loss,

whereas

phagolysosomal digestion executes corpse disposal.

Keeping these two concepts separate is essential.

---

## Protective Checkpoints Against Inappropriate Efferocytosis

Several mechanisms prevent viable cells from being accidentally engulfed.

The major systems include:

maintenance of PS on the inner membrane leaflet by flippases such as ATP11C,

absence of apoptotic find-me signals,

and CD47–SIRPα inhibitory signaling.

Thus:

healthy cell → ATP11C maintains lipid asymmetry + low extracellular PS + CD47–SIRPα inhibitory signal → no engulfment.

During apoptosis:

caspases disable lipid asymmetry + increase PS exposure → eat-me signaling exceeds inhibitory protection → clearance becomes favored.

This is a recognition-state transition rather than a death-execution transition.

---

## Failed LAP Does Not Rescue the Target Cell

Loss of LAP machinery can impair corpse degradation and alter inflammatory responses.

However:

LAP deficiency → defective processing of already engulfed corpses,

not

prevention of upstream apoptosis.

This distinction is particularly important when interpreting ATG-related genes.

Finding ATG5, ATG7, LC3, Rubicon, or VPS34 dependence during efferocytosis does not mean the target underwent autophagy-dependent cell death.

These genes are operating in the **phagocyte** to process an external corpse.

---

## Experimental Validation

A rigorous efferocytosis experiment should separate at least four processes:

target-cell death,

target-cell recognition,

corpse internalization,

and intracellular digestion.

The upstream death program should first be independently validated.

For apoptotic targets this may include caspase activity, apoptotic morphology, mitochondrial changes, and other pathway-specific markers.

Then clearance competence can be tested.

Target-side manipulations include:

XKR8 perturbation,

ATP11C perturbation,

and controlled alteration of PS exposure.

Phagocyte-side manipulations include:

TIM4,

MerTK,

other PS-receptor systems,

ELMO/DOCK180,

Rac1,

LAP components,

TRPM7,

and V-ATPase.

This permits separation of:

death,

recognition,

uptake,

and degradation.

---

## Measuring Engulfment Versus Association

A major experimental problem is distinguishing apoptotic cells attached to phagocytes from apoptotic cells truly internalized.

Microscopy, imaging flow cytometry, fluorescence-quenching strategies, or differential intracellular/extracellular labeling can distinguish these states.

The logic should be:

target bound to outer macrophage membrane ≠ engulfed target.

Target enclosed within phagocyte membrane = internalization.

Internalized target within acidified compartment = successful maturation.

These represent progressively stronger evidence of complete efferocytosis.

---

## Measuring Efferosome Acidification

Tandem-fluorescent probes and pH-sensitive reporters can identify the transition from extracellular or early phagosomal material to acidic intracellular compartments [51-52].

A useful experimental sequence is:

fluorescent apoptotic target → macrophage uptake → pH-dependent reporter shift → quantification of acidified corpse-containing compartments.

This distinguishes mere internalization from productive degradation.

---

## Causal Assignment

The strongest causal design is:

induce a defined death program in the target → confirm death independently → manipulate target-cell PS presentation → measure receptor-dependent recognition → manipulate Rac1 pathway → quantify internalization → manipulate LAP/TRPM7/V-ATPase → quantify acidification and degradation.

For example:

XKR8 loss → reduced PS presentation → impaired recognition.

MerTK/TIM4 disruption → reduced receptor-dependent uptake.

Rac1 inhibition → recognition preserved but engulfment impaired.

TRPM7/V-ATPase/LAP disruption → uptake may occur but maturation/digestion fails.

This stepwise experimental architecture maps molecules to their actual positions in the pathway.

---

## Distinction from Ordinary Phagocytosis

Ordinary phagocytosis is a broad process by which cells internalize extracellular particles, microorganisms or cellular material.

Efferocytosis is a specialized form of phagocytic clearance centered on dying-self recognition.

Its distinguishing features include:

death-associated find-me signaling,

PS-centered eat-me recognition,

specialized efferocytic receptors,

rapid corpse uptake,

and strong immunoregulatory/metabolic consequences.

Therefore:

all efferocytosis is phagocytic,

but not all phagocytosis is efferocytosis.

---

## Distinction from Entosis

Efferocytosis should also be separated conceptually from processes in which living cells become internalized.

During canonical efferocytosis:

the target is already dying/dead.

The phagocyte clears it.

The engulfment process is therefore downstream of death.

This distinguishes efferocytosis from cell-in-cell processes in which internalization itself may contribute causally to the fate of a living target.

---

## Distinction from Apoptosis

The relationship is sequential rather than synonymous.

Apoptosis:

death stimulus → apoptotic machinery → irreversible loss of viability.

Efferocytosis:

apoptotic signaling → PS/find-me signals → recognition → engulfment → degradation.

Apoptosis can occur without immediate efferocytosis.

Efferocytosis generally requires a recognizable dying or dead target but does not create the canonical apoptotic execution program.

Therefore, the two should remain separate entities.

---

## Distinction from Secondary Necrosis

Secondary necrosis is what can happen when apoptotic corpses are not removed efficiently.

Thus:

apoptosis → successful efferocytosis → intracellular corpse degradation.

Versus:

apoptosis → failed clearance → secondary necrosis → membrane rupture.

Efferocytosis therefore acts primarily to **prevent** secondary necrotic progression.

---

## Distinction from Necroptosis

Necroptosis kills through:

RIPK3 → MLKL → plasma-membrane injury.

Efferocytosis does not.

A necroptotic corpse can subsequently become an efferocytic substrate.

Thus:

necroptosis = death mechanism.

Efferocytosis = subsequent clearance mechanism.

Any PS exposure shared between them represents convergence at corpse recognition, not shared execution machinery.

---

## Distinction from Pyroptosis

Pyroptosis kills through gasdermin pore formation and is typically associated with inflammatory mediator release.

Efferocytosis instead internalizes the remaining dying/dead material.

Thus:

gasdermin activation → pyroptotic death,

followed potentially by:

PS recognition → phagocytic clearance.

Again, execution and clearance are different biological levels.

---

## Molecular Hierarchy

The pathway is best divided between **target-cell preparation** and **phagocyte execution of clearance**.

### Upstream death machinery

Depends on the original RCD.

For apoptotic targets:

caspases,

BAX/BAK,

caspase-8,

caspase-9,

caspase-3/7,

and other apoptotic proteins may determine target death.

These are not efferocytosis-specific executors.

### Target-side clearance signals

XKR8,

ATP11C,

pannexin-1,

phosphatidylserine,

ATP/UTP,

LPC,

S1P,

CX3CL1.

### Phagocyte recruitment receptors

P2Y2,

G2A,

S1P receptors,

CX3CR1.

### Corpse-recognition systems

TIM4,

BAI1,

stabilins,

CD300-family receptors,

CD14-associated systems,

MerTK/TAM receptors,

αv integrins.

### Bridging molecules

Gas6,

Protein S,

MFG-E8.

### Engulfment transducers

TRIO,

RhoG,

ELMO,

DOCK180,

Src,

FAK,

p130CAS,

CrkII,

Rac1,

### Efferosome-maturation machinery

### Negative regulators

There is **no efferocytosis-specific molecule whose primary function is to execute death of the canonical target cell**.

---

## Integrated Canonical Sequence

The complete canonical sequence can be represented as:

upstream apoptotic stimulus → apoptotic commitment and caspase activation.

Then the dying cell acquires a clearance phenotype:

caspase-3/7 → XKR8 activation + ATP11C inactivation → stable PS externalization.

In parallel:

caspase-associated pannexin-1 activation → ATP/UTP release → P2Y2-dependent phagocyte recruitment.

Additional context-dependent signals may include:

LPC → G2A,

S1P → S1PR,

CX3CL1 → CX3CR1.

The phagocyte reaches the dying cell:

externalized PS → direct TIM4/BAI1/stabilin/CD300-type recognition

and/or

PS → Gas6/Protein S → TAM receptors

and/or

PS → MFG-E8 → αv-integrin signaling.

Recognition is balanced against:

CD47 → SIRPα → SHP1/SHP2 → don't-eat-me inhibition.

If pro-engulfment signals dominate:

receptor engagement → TRIO → RhoG → ELMO–DOCK180 → Rac1,

with parallel MerTK/Src/FAK/p130CAS/CrkII/DOCK180 signaling and Ca²⁺-dependent cytoskeletal control.

Then:

Rac1-dependent actin remodeling → phagocytic-cup extension → circumferential membrane enclosure → cup closure → corpse internalization into a single-membrane efferosome.

Post-engulfment:

efferosome → VPS34/PI3P signaling → Rab5-to-Rab7 maturation + LAP/Rubicon/ATG5/ATG7/LC3 recruitment + TRPM7-associated Ca²⁺ signaling → V-ATPase-dependent acidification → lysosomal fusion → lysosomal hydrolase-mediated degradation.

Finally:

corpse-derived metabolites → efferocyte metabolic/epigenetic reprogramming → enhanced clearance capacity + tissue-resolution or context-dependent immunoregulatory signaling.

---

## Efficient Versus Failed Clearance

### Successful pathway

apoptosis → PS/find-me signaling → recognition → Rac1-dependent engulfment → LAP/Rab/V-ATPase-dependent maturation → lysosomal corpse degradation → contained disposal → resolution-associated signaling.

### Failed pathway

apoptosis → PS exposure → ineffective recognition or uptake → extracellular persistence of apoptotic corpse → membrane-repair capacity exhausted → secondary necrosis → membrane rupture → DAMP release → inflammation.

Thus, the critical physiological outcome controlled by efferocytosis is not whether apoptosis occurred.

It is whether the dead cell is removed **before it becomes an inflammatory extracellular corpse**.

---

## Final Mechanistic Interpretation

Efferocytosis is best defined as a **regulated clearance and immunometabolic program downstream of cell-death commitment**, not as an autonomous regulated cell-death modality.

Its canonical target is an apoptotic cell.

The target's death machinery first generates the recognition state:

apoptosis → caspase activation → XKR8-mediated phospholipid scrambling + ATP11C inactivation → phosphatidylserine exposure,

while pannexin-1-dependent nucleotide release and other context-dependent find-me signals recruit phagocytes.

Recognition then occurs through a combinatorial PS-receptor system involving direct receptors such as TIM4 and BAI1 or bridging systems such as Gas6/Protein S–TAM receptors and MFG-E8–integrins.

These pathways converge on:

TRIO/RhoG/ELMO/DOCK180 and related signaling → Rac1 → actin remodeling → phagocytic-cup formation → internalization.

The corpse is then processed through:

VPS34/PI3P → Rab maturation + LC3-associated phagocytosis/Rubicon → TRPM7-associated Ca²⁺ signaling → V-ATPase-dependent acidification → lysosomal degradation.

The principal conceptual distinction is therefore:

**caspases or another RCD-specific machinery cause loss of viability; the efferocytic machinery subsequently causes recognition, engulfment, and destruction of the corpse.**

There is consequently no valid efferocytosis-specific point of no return for target-cell death.

Stable PS exposure may mark commitment to recognition, and complete phagocytic-cup closure marks commitment to intracellular disposal, but neither constitutes the original lethal event.

The most important biological consequence of efficient efferocytosis is prevention of secondary necrosis and inflammatory release of intracellular material.

When clearance fails, apoptotic corpses can progressively lose membrane integrity and undergo secondary necrosis.

When clearance succeeds, the dying cell is removed in a controlled intracellular compartment and its components are metabolically recycled.

Accordingly, within a mechanistic atlas of regulated cell death, efferocytosis should be positioned **after** the execution phase as a downstream fate of dying cells rather than alongside apoptosis, ferroptosis, necroptosis, or other autonomous death modalities.

Its most defensible operational definition is:

**a regulated phagocytic process in which dying-cell-derived find-me and eat-me signals trigger receptor-dependent Rac1-mediated engulfment and phagolysosomal degradation of the corpse, thereby controlling inflammatory consequences and tissue recovery without constituting the original mechanism that kills the target cell.**
