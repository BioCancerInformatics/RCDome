# Floatptosis

## Definition and Classification

Floatptosis is a **newly proposed and still incompletely consolidated form of regulated cell death characterized by pathogen-induced, SLC9A9-dependent fusion and enlargement of late endosomal/lysosomal vacuoles** in macrophages [1-2].

It was described in 2025 in the context of infection with a virulent *Bergeyella cardium* variant and was subsequently discussed among emerging regulated cell-death modalities [1-2].

The currently supported sequence is:

**Bergeyella cardium variant infection or bacterial OMV/β-barrel-protein exposure → uptake/delivery of bacterial cargo → unknown proximal host-sensing step → engagement of SLC9A9-positive endolysosomal compartments → abnormal late endosome/lysosome fusion → formation of giant Rab7⁺/LAMP1⁺ single-membrane vacuoles → progressive occupation and compression of the cytoplasm → disruption of trafficking and membrane/homeostatic control → plasma-membrane permeabilization → death.**

The most important classification caveat is that floatptosis is **not yet an established canonical NCCD modality** [13].

Therefore, the most defensible RCDome classification is:

**Emerging/candidate regulated cell-death modality**

with:

**Evidence status: provisional / early-stage**

and

**Primary biological context: pathogen-induced macrophage death.**

---

# Core Concept

Floatptosis is unusual because its proposed execution logic centers on **pathological organelle fusion** rather than a dedicated proteolytic or pore-forming death executor.

The core concept is:

**bacterial manipulation of host endolysosomal trafficking → uncontrolled fusion of late endosomal/lysosomal compartments → giant vacuoles → loss of cellular architecture and membrane homeostasis → death.**

Thus, in the current model:

SLC9A9 is not merely a biomarker.

It is a functionally required host component.

By contrast, no equivalent of:

caspase-3/7,

BAX/BAK,

MLKL,

GSDMD,

or GPX4 failure

has yet been identified as the dedicated terminal executor.

---

# Biological Context

## Bergeyella cardium Variant

The best-established biological trigger is a pathogenic *Bergeyella cardium* variant [1].

Exposure of macrophages to this bacterium produces extensive cytoplasmic vacuolization followed by loss of plasma-membrane integrity and cell death.

The proposed sequence begins:

**BCV infection → delivery of pathogen-derived vacuolization factors → remodeling of host endolysosomal compartments → floatptosis.**

This makes floatptosis fundamentally different from many cell-autonomous stress pathways.

Its currently established trigger is **microbial**.

---

# Outer Membrane Vesicles

Gram-negative bacteria release outer membrane vesicles, or OMVs.

OMVs can transport:

outer-membrane proteins,

lipids,

enzymes,

virulence factors,

and other bacterial cargo

into host cells [3-5].

In the floatptosis model:

**BCV → OMVs → macrophage uptake → intracellular delivery of bacterial components → vacuolization.**

Purified bacterial vesicle material can reproduce important aspects of the phenotype.

This strongly supports the idea that whole intracellular bacterial replication is not required to generate the death program.

---

# Bacterial β-Barrel Proteins

A particularly important observation is that selected bacterial outer-membrane proteins can reproduce the vacuolization phenotype [1].

The implicated proteins include:

lipocalin-associated components,

β-barrel proteins,

and PorV-associated bacterial membrane proteins.

The functional sequence is:

**bacterial β-barrel/barrel-associated protein → intracellular host-cell activity → endolysosomal remodeling → giant vacuole formation.**

This indicates that specific bacterial proteins are sufficient to engage the host pathway.

However, the direct host binding partners remain unknown.

---

# The Proximal Host Sensor Is Unknown

No dedicated mammalian receptor has yet been established as the floatptosis sensor.

Therefore, the pathway should not currently be written as:

bacterial protein → receptor X → SLC9A9.

The defensible sequence is:

**OMV/bacterial-protein uptake → unknown intracellular sensing or trafficking interaction → SLC9A9-dependent endolysosomal response.**

The missing connection between bacterial cargo and SLC9A9-associated vacuole fusion is one of the principal mechanistic gaps.

---

# OMV Uptake Is Not Sufficient to Define Floatptosis

Bacterial OMVs can induce several different forms of host-cell stress and death.

For example, OMVs from *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* can deliver PorB to host mitochondria and activate intrinsic apoptosis [3-4].

Thus:

**OMV exposure ≠ floatptosis.**

The defining phenotype requires the specific endolysosomal/vacuolar mechanism.

This distinction is particularly important because bacterial vesicles can activate:

apoptosis,

inflammation,

innate immune signaling,

mitochondrial damage,

or vacuolization

depending on the bacterial species and cargo.

---

# SLC9A9 as the Central Host Node

The strongest host factor currently implicated in floatptosis is **SLC9A9**, also known as **NHE9** [1].

SLC9A9 belongs to the intracellular Na⁺/H⁺ exchanger family [6,11].

Its general biochemical activity is:

**luminal H⁺ ↔ cytosolic Na⁺/K⁺ exchange.**

Therefore, SLC9A9 influences:

endosomal pH,

ionic composition,

membrane trafficking,

organelle maturation,

and fusion competence.

This makes it biologically plausible that abnormal SLC9A9 activity could substantially alter endolysosomal dynamics.

---

# SLC9A9 Is Not a Classical Death Receptor

SLC9A9 should not be represented as:

a death receptor,

a protease,

or

a terminal membrane pore.

Its function is better described as:

**an endosomal ion-transport regulator whose activity becomes necessary for pathogen-induced pathological organelle fusion.**

Thus:

**SLC9A9 → permissive endolysosomal physicochemical state → abnormal fusion → vacuolar death phenotype.**

---

# Endosomal Na⁺/H⁺ Exchange

The endosomal lumen is acidified primarily through proton-pumping systems such as V-ATPases.

Na⁺/H⁺ exchangers counterbalance this acidification by exporting luminal protons.

Therefore:

**V-ATPase → luminal acidification**

while

**SLC9A9/NHE9 → proton leakage/counter-ion exchange.**

The balance determines organellar pH.

This matters because vesicular fusion machinery is highly sensitive to membrane composition, luminal state, ion concentrations, and protein recruitment.

---

# Lessons from Nhx1

Mechanistic support for a relationship between Na⁺/H⁺ exchange and vacuole fusion comes from the yeast homolog Nhx1 [7-8].

Nhx1 regulates vacuolar fusion.

Loss of exchanger activity disrupts efficient fusion initiation.

These findings support a general principle:

**endosomal/vacuolar ion exchange → lumenal pH and membrane state → fusion competence.**

However, the exact yeast intermediates should not be automatically assigned to mammalian floatptosis.

They provide biological context rather than direct proof.

---

# SLC9A9 and Macrophage Phagosomal Biology

SLC9A9/NHE9 also regulates macrophage phagosomal maturation [9].

Changes in NHE9 activity can alter:

organelle pH,

vesicle trafficking,

motor recruitment,

lysosomal interactions,

and bactericidal activity.

This reinforces the biological plausibility that pathogenic manipulation of SLC9A9 could reprogram macrophage endolysosomal behavior.

Nevertheless:

normal NHE9-mediated phagosome maturation ≠ floatptosis.

Floatptosis requires pathological, death-associated vacuolar remodeling.

---

# First Defined Intracellular Transition

The earliest host intracellular transition currently supported is therefore not:

caspase cleavage,

mitochondrial outer-membrane permeabilization,

or kinase-driven necroptosis.

Instead, it is:

**SLC9A9-associated alteration of endolysosomal pH/trafficking state → increased pathological fusion competence.**

This makes floatptosis mechanistically unusual among emerging RCDs.

---

# Late Endosome Identity

The vacuoles involved in floatptosis are associated with late endosomal markers.

Rab7 is especially important as a marker of late endosomal identity.

Thus:

**pathogen-induced trafficking perturbation → Rab7⁺ compartment enlargement/fusion.**

Rab7 positivity helps identify the organelle origin.

However:

**Rab7 = compartment marker/regulator**

rather than:

**Rab7 = dedicated floatptosis executor.**

---

# Lysosomal Identity

LAMP1 positivity indicates strong lysosomal or late endolysosomal identity of the expanding compartments [1-2].

Therefore, the characteristic vacuole is:

**Rab7⁺ / LAMP1⁺**

rather than an:

ER-derived,

mitochondrial-derived,

or purely macropinosomal compartment.

This organelle origin is crucial for distinguishing floatptosis from other vacuolization-associated deaths.

---

# Single-Membrane Vacuoles

The large vacuoles are best understood as enlarged/fused components of the endolysosomal system.

They are not described as canonical double-membrane autophagosomes.

Therefore:

**large vacuole ≠ autophagosome by default.**

This prevents the erroneous conclusion that dramatic vacuolization automatically indicates autophagy-dependent cell death.

---

# Pathological Vacuole Fusion

The central structural event is repeated membrane fusion.

A generic vesicular fusion process normally involves:

Rab-dependent identity and tethering,

SNARE complex assembly,

membrane juxtaposition,

and lipid bilayer merger.

Floatptosis appears to exploit or dysregulate this normal machinery.

The proposed transition is:

**many normal-sized endolysosomal compartments → repeated fusion → fewer but much larger vacuoles.**

As this continues, vacuoles occupy an increasing proportion of cytoplasmic volume.

---

# Fusion Rather Than Simple Swelling

This distinction is important.

The phenotype is not best described simply as:

lysosome swelling.

The evidence instead supports:

**fusion-dependent enlargement.**

Thus:

vacuole A + vacuole B → enlarged vacuole

followed by:

enlarged vacuole + additional compartment → giant vacuole.

This repeated fusion gradually produces the characteristic floatptotic morphology.

---

# Cytoplasmic Compression

As giant vacuoles expand, functional cytoplasm becomes displaced and compressed.

The sequence becomes:

**vacuole fusion → increasing vacuolar volume → shrinking effective cytoplasmic space.**

This can interfere with:

organelle positioning,

cytoskeletal organization,

membrane trafficking,

metabolic reactions,

protein synthesis,

and intracellular transport.

The cell can therefore become dysfunctional even before complete membrane permeabilization occurs.

---

# Disruption of Intracellular Trafficking

The endosomal and lysosomal systems normally depend on highly regulated compartment identity.

Pathological fusion undermines that organization.

Thus:

**excessive fusion → loss of normal compartmentalization → trafficking failure.**

Potential consequences include disruption of:

cargo recycling,

receptor degradation,

phagosome maturation,

nutrient sensing,

membrane turnover,

and lysosomal positioning.

These downstream consequences are mechanistically plausible, although not every one has been demonstrated directly in floatptosis.

---

# Lysosome-Associated Homeostatic Collapse

Because the lysosomal system integrates:

degradation,

recycling,

nutrient signaling,

autophagic cargo processing,

and membrane trafficking,

massive structural remodeling can affect whole-cell homeostasis.

The emerging model is therefore:

**pathological lysosomal/endosomal fusion → organelle-network collapse → global cellular dysfunction.**

This provides a mechanistic bridge between vacuole formation and death.

---

# Vacuolization Precedes Plasma-Membrane Damage

One of the most important temporal observations is:

**large-vacuole formation occurs before terminal membrane permeabilization** [1-2].

Therefore, membrane damage should not be placed at the start of the pathway.

The chronology is:

normal macrophage → vacuole appearance → vacuole fusion/enlargement → massive cytoplasmic vacuolization → plasma-membrane permeabilization → death.

This ordering supports a causal role for vacuolar remodeling.

---

# Amiloride Sensitivity

Amiloride provides important pharmacological evidence [1].

Amiloride and related compounds can inhibit Na⁺/H⁺ exchanger activity.

In the floatptosis model:

**amiloride → suppression of SLC9-associated transport → reduction of vacuolization → reduction of cell death.**

This provides functional evidence that the exchanger-associated mechanism occurs upstream of death.

---

# Amiloride Is a Pathway Probe, Not a Specific Biomarker

Amiloride affects several Na⁺/H⁺ exchange processes.

Therefore, sensitivity to amiloride alone is insufficient to classify a death event as floatptosis.

The stronger mechanistic combination is:

pathogen/OMV/β-barrel trigger

*

Rab7⁺/LAMP1⁺ giant vacuoles

*

SLC9A9 requirement

*

amiloride sensitivity

*

vacuolization preceding membrane permeabilization.

---

# Genetic Evidence for SLC9A9

SLC9A9 deficiency suppresses the floatptosis-associated phenotype [1].

This is stronger than simple correlative expression.

The causal logic is:

**BCV/OMV trigger → SLC9A9 present → giant vacuolization → death.**

Versus:

**BCV/OMV trigger → SLC9A9 deficient → reduced vacuole formation → increased cellular/host resistance.**

Thus, SLC9A9 currently represents the strongest genetically supported host node.

---

# SLC9A9 as a Vulnerability Factor

SLC9A9 is probably better described as a required **host susceptibility factor** than as a classical terminal executor.

It allows the pathogen-induced endolysosomal remodeling to proceed.

Therefore:

**SLC9A9 activity → permissive condition for floatptosis.**

Without this permissive state, the vacuolar phenotype and death are substantially reduced.

---

# Potential V-ATPase Interaction

Endosomal pH reflects a balance between proton influx and proton efflux.

V-ATPase pumps protons into compartments.

SLC9A9 counterbalances this process.

Thus, a mechanistically reasonable framework is:

**V-ATPase proton pumping ↔ SLC9A9 proton leakage → optimal endosomal pH range.**

Disruption of this balance could alter membrane-fusion competence.

However, direct evidence proving a specific V-ATPase–SLC9A9 circuit as the floatptosis trigger remains incomplete.

V-ATPase should therefore be treated as **supporting endolysosomal physiology**, not a proven pathway-specific executor.

---

# Candidate Fusion Machinery

General late-endosome/lysosome fusion involves proteins including:

Rab GTPases,

tethering complexes,

and SNAREs.

Because giant vacuoles clearly arise through organelle fusion, these proteins are likely required at some level.

However, the currently supplied evidence does not identify a unique:

Rab,

SNARE,

or tethering complex

as the defining floatptosis transducer.

Therefore, they should not yet be placed into the canonical pathway as specific obligatory molecular nodes.

The correct representation is:

**SLC9A9-dependent endolysosomal state → host membrane-fusion machinery → pathological vacuole enlargement.**

---

# Candidate Commitment Point

No universal molecular point of no return has been established.

Early vacuole formation may remain reversible.

Therefore:

**one enlarged vacuole ≠ commitment to death.**

The strongest operational candidate for commitment is the transition toward:

**widespread fused-vacuole occupation of the cytoplasm + irreversible trafficking/homeostatic failure + subsequent plasma-membrane permeabilization.**

This represents a late structural commitment rather than a discrete biochemical switch.

---

# Distributed Commitment

Floatptosis may therefore resemble other vacuolization-associated death processes in which commitment emerges gradually.

For example:

initial fusion → potentially manageable,

continued fusion → cytoplasmic displacement,

more fusion → trafficking collapse,

giant-vacuole occupation → severe structural dysfunction,

membrane permeabilization → terminal state.

Thus:

**commitment may result from cumulative organelle-system failure rather than one molecular activation event.**

---

# What Physically Kills the Cell?

The precise answer remains unresolved.

The strongest current interpretation is:

**catastrophic endolysosomal remodeling eventually causes failure of global membrane and cellular homeostasis.**

The data do not establish:

a dedicated floatptosis membrane pore,

a floatptosis-specific protease,

a lipid-peroxidative executor,

or a single lysosomal hydrolase that directly kills the cell.

Therefore, the terminal mechanism should not be overstated.

---

# Possible Mechanical Contribution

Giant vacuoles occupy substantial intracellular volume.

This can:

compress the cytosol,

displace organelles,

distort the plasma membrane,

and alter membrane tension.

A plausible sequence is:

**giant vacuole expansion → intracellular mechanical pressure → plasma-membrane stress → permeability failure.**

This is mechanistically reasonable but not yet directly proven as the exclusive terminal lesion.

---

# Possible Osmotic and Ionic Contribution

Large endolysosomal compartments contain ions, protons, solutes, proteins, and water.

Pathological fusion can therefore alter:

intracellular osmotic balance,

ion distribution,

proton gradients,

and membrane potentials.

Thus:

**fusion-driven compartment expansion → ionic/osmotic disequilibrium → energetic demand and membrane instability.**

Again, these processes likely contribute but have not yet been resolved into one terminal causal chain.

---

# Possible Trafficking Failure

An alternative or complementary terminal mechanism is:

**endolysosomal fusion consumes functional vesicle pools → normal membrane trafficking collapses → plasma-membrane maintenance fails.**

Cells continuously require endosomal recycling to maintain plasma-membrane composition.

If this system is pathologically diverted into giant fused vacuoles, the plasma membrane could eventually lose its ability to maintain integrity.

This remains an attractive but incompletely demonstrated mechanism.

---

# Plasma-Membrane Permeabilization

The terminal phenotype includes loss of membrane integrity.

The correct temporal relationship is:

**vacuolization first → membrane permeabilization later.**

This distinguishes the pathway from deaths initiated by direct plasma-membrane pore formation.

Thus:

membrane permeabilization is likely a **downstream terminal consequence** rather than the primary initiating lesion.

---

# No Dedicated Pore-Forming Executor Has Been Identified

At present, no evidence establishes:

gasdermin,

MLKL,

NINJ1,

or another dedicated pore-forming protein

as the central floatptosis executor.

Therefore, the pathway should not be drawn as:

SLC9A9 → pore protein → death.

The defensible sequence remains:

**SLC9A9-dependent vacuolar fusion → organelle/homeostatic collapse → secondary membrane permeabilization.**

---

# Relationship with Bacterial OMV-Induced Apoptosis

Bacterial OMVs can independently induce intrinsic apoptosis [3-5].

For example, *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* OMVs can deliver PorB to mitochondria [3].

A representative pathway is:

**OMV uptake → PorB targeting to mitochondria → mitochondrial dysfunction → intrinsic apoptosis.**

Subsequent studies also identified:

MCL-1 depletion,

BAK-associated mitochondrial damage,

cytochrome c release,

and caspase-3/7 activation [4].

This is not floatptosis.

---

# Parallel Apoptotic Crosstalk

Thus, bacterial OMV exposure can produce at least two conceptually different outcomes.

### Classical OMV-associated intrinsic apoptosis

bacterial cargo → mitochondria → BAK/MCL-1-associated mitochondrial failure → cytochrome c → caspase cascade → apoptosis.

### Floatptosis

BCV-derived cargo → endolysosomal system → SLC9A9-dependent pathological vacuole fusion → giant LAMP1⁺/Rab7⁺ vacuoles → membrane/homeostatic collapse.

These mechanisms should remain separated experimentally.

---

# Distinction from Apoptosis

Canonical apoptosis uses:

intrinsic stress → BAX/BAK → MOMP → cytochrome c → APAF1/caspase-9 → caspase-3/7

or

death receptor → caspase-8 → caspase-3/7.

Floatptosis does not currently have this execution architecture.

The defining structural event is endolysosomal vacuole fusion.

Therefore:

**caspase activation alone would argue for an apoptotic component rather than prove floatptosis.**

---

# Distinction from Pyroptosis

Pyroptosis involves:

inflammatory signaling → gasdermin activation → plasma-membrane pores → osmotic swelling → inflammatory release and eventual membrane rupture.

Floatptosis instead begins intracellularly with:

**pathological late-endosome/lysosome fusion.**

Therefore:

pyroptosis → primary pore-forming execution.

floatptosis → primary vacuolar/endolysosomal remodeling with late secondary membrane permeabilization.

---

# Distinction from Necroptosis

Necroptosis is defined by:

RIPK3 → MLKL → membrane-associated MLKL oligomers → ionic disruption and membrane failure.

No comparable RIPK3–MLKL machinery defines floatptosis.

Thus:

**floatptosis ≠ vacuolated necroptosis.**

---

# Distinction from Ferroptosis

Ferroptosis is driven by:

iron-dependent phospholipid peroxidation → membrane lipid damage → terminal permeability failure.

No defining lipid-peroxidation mechanism has been demonstrated in floatptosis.

Floatptosis is a newly described vacuolization-associated regulated cell death and a differential diagnosis of vacuolizing/lytic cell death [1].

Both involve dramatic cytoplasmic vacuolization.

However, the origin of the vacuoles differs.

### Methuosis

macropinocytosis → dysfunctional macropinosomes/late endosomes → failure of normal maturation/recycling → giant poorly degradative vacuoles → metabolic/homeostatic failure.

### Floatptosis

bacterial trigger → SLC9A9-dependent late-endosome/lysosome fusion → Rab7⁺/LAMP1⁺ fused vacuoles → terminal death.

Methuosis is a type of nonapoptotic cytoplasmic vacuolization cell death marked by the vacuolization of late endosome and lysosome [1,12].

However, paraptotic vacuoles generally arise from:

ER dilation,

and/or

mitochondrial swelling.

Mechanisms can involve:

proteostasis stress,

Ca²⁺ imbalance,

ROS,

MAPK/JNK signaling,

and ER stress,

depending on the model.

Floatptosis instead centers on:

**late endosome/lysosome-derived LAMP1⁺/Rab7⁺ vacuoles.**

Therefore:

**organelle origin is one of the strongest discriminators.**

---

# Distinction from Lysosomal Swelling Induced by Drugs

Other experimental systems can also produce giant lysosomal vacuoles.

For example, abemaciclib has been reported to induce atypical vacuolization-associated death involving lysosomal compartments [10].

Therefore:

**LAMP1-positive vacuoles alone ≠ floatptosis.**

The defining floatptosis context additionally requires:

the pathogen/OMV/β-barrel trigger

and

SLC9A9 dependence.

---

# Morphological Sequence

The characteristic chronological morphology is:

**normal macrophage → appearance of numerous endolysosomal vacuoles → increasing Rab7⁺/LAMP1⁺ vacuoles → repeated vacuole fusion → formation of very large single-membrane vacuoles → displacement and compression of remaining cytoplasm → severe intracellular architectural distortion → plasma-membrane permeabilization → death.**

This morphology is striking but not sufficient by itself.

Vacuolization is a shared phenotype across multiple emerging death mechanisms.

---

# Why Morphology Alone Is Insufficient

Large cytoplasmic vacuoles can be seen in:

floatptosis,

methuosis,

paraptosis,

drug-induced lysosomal injury,

autophagic perturbations,

and infectious cellular stress.

Therefore, a convincing mechanistic assignment requires more than microscopy.

The key question is:

**where do the vacuoles come from, why do they fuse, and which molecular perturbation prevents the death?**

---

# Experimental Validation

The strongest available evidence uses a combination of:

pathogen phenocopy,

purified bacterial material,

specific bacterial-protein expression,

genetic host-factor perturbation,

pharmacological rescue,

organelle markers,

and temporal analysis.

The core causal sequence should be tested at multiple levels.

---

# Trigger Validation

Researchers should demonstrate that:

BCV infection induces the phenotype,

and/or

BCV-derived OMVs reproduce it,

and/or

specific bacterial β-barrel/barrel-associated proteins reproduce it.

This establishes that the bacterial material is sufficient.

---

# Compartment Origin

The vacuoles should be characterized using markers.

The key current phenotype is:

**Rab7 positive**

and

**LAMP1 positive.**

This supports late-endosome/lysosome origin.

Markers of:

ER,

mitochondria,

macropinosomes,

and autophagosomes

can help exclude alternative vacuolar deaths.

---

# SLC9A9 Dependency

The strongest host-mechanism experiment is disruption of SLC9A9.

The causal prediction is:

**SLC9A9 loss → reduced giant-vacuole fusion → reduced floatptotic death.**

This establishes that the exchanger is mechanistically upstream of the lethal phenotype.

---

# Pharmacological Rescue

Amiloride-sensitive inhibition provides complementary evidence.

A mechanistically strong result is:

**amiloride treatment → reduced vacuolization + improved viability.**

Pharmacological results are strongest when they agree with genetic SLC9A9 perturbation.

---

# Temporal Ordering

Time-course imaging should establish:

**vacuole fusion before membrane permeabilization.**

This is essential.

If membrane damage consistently preceded vacuolization, the current causal model would be weakened.

Instead, the existing framework supports:

**vacuolar remodeling → terminal membrane failure.**

---

# Exclusion of Competing RCDs

A rigorous classification should test major alternatives.

For apoptosis:

BAX/BAK,

MOMP,

cytochrome c,

caspases.

For pyroptosis:

inflammasome/caspase activation,

gasdermins.

For necroptosis:

RIPK3,

MLKL.

For ferroptosis:

lipid peroxidation,

GPX4-associated dependence,

iron sensitivity.

For methuosis:

macropinocytic origin and Rab5/Rab7-associated abnormal macropinosome behavior.

For paraptosis:

ER/mitochondrial swelling and its characteristic signaling context.

---

# Point of No Return

No MOMP-like biochemical switch has been defined.

The earliest vacuolar events may be reversible.

A reasonable working model is:

### Early stage

bacterial cargo → SLC9A9-associated endolysosomal perturbation → small or moderate vacuoles.

Potentially reversible.

### Progressive stage

repeated fusion → giant vacuoles → severe displacement of functional cytoplasm.

Increasingly difficult to reverse.

### Candidate commitment stage

widespread endolysosomal remodeling causes irreversible failure of vesicular, ionic, metabolic, and membrane homeostasis.

### Terminal stage

plasma-membrane permeability increases → viability is lost.

Thus, the point of no return is probably a **systems-level threshold**, not a single known protein modification.

---

# Protective Mechanisms

## SLC9A9 Loss

The strongest direct protective intervention is genetic loss or reduction of SLC9A9 [1].

Thus:

**SLC9A9 deficiency → reduced pathological vacuole fusion → resistance.**

---

# Pharmacological Na⁺/H⁺ Exchange Inhibition

Amiloride-associated inhibition similarly suppresses the pathway.

Therefore:

**Na⁺/H⁺ exchanger activity = actionable vulnerability.**

However, because amiloride can affect multiple exchangers, SLC9A9-specific genetic evidence remains particularly important.

---

# Maintenance of Normal Endolysosomal Identity

Conceptually, protective mechanisms should preserve:

correct organellar pH,

controlled Rab identity,

normal fusion specificity,

normal SNARE activity,

and appropriate lysosome encounter frequency.

These are biologically plausible checkpoints.

However, specific floatptosis-protective proteins in these systems remain incompletely defined.

---

# Potential Role of Normal pH Control

Because SLC9A9 regulates proton flux, a balanced endosomal pH likely protects against pathological membrane behavior.

The general principle is:

**normal proton pumping + normal proton leakage → controlled endolysosomal maturation.**

Pathogen-associated distortion of this balance may create:

**fusion-permissive abnormal compartment state → floatptosis.**

The exact pH threshold has not yet been determined.

---

# Relationship to Host Defense

Interestingly, SLC9A9 deficiency not only suppresses vacuolization but can improve host resistance in the reported system [1].

This suggests that the bacterial pathway may exploit a normal macrophage trafficking protein for pathogenic benefit.

Thus:

**bacterial factor → hijacking of host SLC9A9-dependent membrane biology → macrophage death → impaired host defense.**

Floatptosis may therefore represent both:

a regulated host-cell death mechanism,

and

a microbial virulence strategy.

---

# Biological Interpretation

The emerging concept is not simply that bacteria damage lysosomes.

It is that pathogenic proteins actively convert a normal trafficking system into a death mechanism.

The cell's own fusion machinery becomes detrimental.

Therefore:

**normal physiological machinery + pathological upstream instruction → regulated lethal remodeling.**

This is mechanistically similar in principle to other RCDs in which normal proteins acquire lethal functions under defined signals.

---

# Transcriptomic Interpretation Requires Caution

Floatptosis is especially poorly suited to diagnosis from transcription alone.

The defining biology includes:

bacterial exposure,

protein trafficking,

organelle pH,

membrane fusion,

vacuole topology,

and compartment identity.

Expression of:

SLC9A9,

RAB7,

LAMP1,

or lysosomal genes

does not demonstrate active floatptosis.

At most, transcriptomic patterns could indicate:

**floatptosis-associated susceptibility**

or

**endolysosomal states compatible with the pathway.**

Direct evidence of active execution requires morphological and functional data.

---

# Molecular Hierarchy

## Initiating agents

*Bergeyella cardium* variant,

BCV-derived outer membrane vesicles,

selected bacterial β-barrel/barrel-associated proteins.

## Proximal receptor/sensor

Unknown.

## Delivery/transduction context

OMV uptake,

endocytic trafficking,

intracellular bacterial-protein activity.

## First established host dependency

**SLC9A9/NHE9.**

## Core biochemical function

Na⁺/H⁺ exchange regulating endosomal ionic and pH state.

## Core organellar machinery

late endosome/lysosome system.

## Core structural phenotype

Rab7⁺/LAMP1⁺ giant single-membrane fused vacuoles.

## Execution process

pathological repeated endolysosomal fusion.

## Candidate commitment state

massive fused-vacuole occupation causing irreversible trafficking and homeostatic collapse.

## Terminal event

secondary plasma-membrane permeabilization and global loss of cellular homeostasis.

## Dedicated terminal executor

**Not established.**

## Negative/protective interventions

SLC9A9 loss,

amiloride-sensitive exchange inhibition.

---

# Evidence Hierarchy

## Comparatively Strong Within the Current Floatptosis Literature

The following observations form the best-supported mechanistic core:

BCV induces macrophage vacuolization and death.

BCV-derived OMVs can reproduce important aspects of the phenotype.

Specific bacterial barrel-associated proteins can induce vacuolization.

The vacuoles display late endosomal/lysosomal identity.

SLC9A9 is functionally required.

Amiloride suppresses vacuole formation/death.

Vacuolization precedes terminal membrane permeability.

---

# Mechanistically Supported but Not Yet Completely Resolved

SLC9A9-dependent pH changes regulate vacuole fusion competence.

Repeated fusion directly causes the lethal cellular state.

Global trafficking collapse contributes to death.

Large vacuoles generate mechanical/ionic stress contributing to plasma-membrane failure.

These are strongly coherent with known endolysosomal biology but need deeper direct testing in floatptosis.

---

# Unresolved Components

The following should remain explicitly unresolved:

the direct macrophage receptor for BCV factors,

the molecular interaction between bacterial proteins and SLC9A9,

whether SLC9A9 transport activity itself is directly modified,

the precise Rab/tether/SNARE machinery executing pathological fusion,

the exact lumenal pH changes,

the quantitative commitment threshold,

the direct physical mechanism of terminal plasma-membrane permeabilization,

and whether floatptosis occurs outside the original pathogen/macrophage context.

---

# Integrated Canonical Sequence

The most defensible integrated sequence begins with:

**virulent Bergeyella cardium variant infection.**

The bacterium releases or delivers:

**outer membrane vesicles containing bacterial membrane proteins and associated cargo.**

These vesicles are taken up by macrophages:

**BCV OMV → host endocytic entry → intracellular delivery of bacterial factors.**

Alternatively, expression/delivery of specific bacterial β-barrel/barrel-associated proteins can reproduce the vacuolar phenotype.

The next molecular step remains unresolved:

**bacterial cargo → unknown host recognition or trafficking interaction.**

The first clearly defined host node is:

**SLC9A9/NHE9-positive endolysosomal compartments.**

SLC9A9 alters proton/Na⁺ or K⁺ exchange across endosomal membranes:

**SLC9A9 activity → altered lumenal ionic/pH environment → altered membrane-fusion competence.**

Pathogen-induced dysregulation then favors:

**late endosome/lysosome fusion.**

The affected compartments retain:

**Rab7⁺ late-endosomal identity**

and

**LAMP1⁺ lysosomal identity.**

Repeated fusion produces:

small vacuoles → larger vacuoles → giant fused single-membrane compartments.

As the process progresses:

**giant vacuoles occupy increasing cytoplasmic volume → residual cytoplasm becomes compressed/displaced → normal endosomal recycling and lysosomal trafficking become disrupted → intracellular organization progressively fails.**

Continued organelle remodeling likely produces combined:

trafficking stress,

ionic disequilibrium,

osmotic stress,

metabolic inefficiency,

and mechanical distortion.

Eventually:

**endolysosomal burden exceeds the cell's ability to maintain membrane and intracellular homeostasis.**

The pathway then progresses to:

**plasma-membrane permeabilization.**

Once permeability becomes irreversible:

ionic gradients collapse → global homeostasis fails → macrophage viability is lost.

Therefore, the current working pathway is:

**BCV/OMV/β-barrel protein → unknown proximal host sensor → SLC9A9-dependent endolysosomal pH/fusion remodeling → Rab7⁺/LAMP1⁺ vacuole fusion → giant vacuolar occupation of cytoplasm → trafficking/homeostatic collapse → secondary plasma-membrane permeabilization → death.**

---

# Candidate Commitment Sequence

A useful operational model is:

### Trigger stage

BCV/OMV/bacterial proteins engage the macrophage.

### Early reversible stage

SLC9A9-dependent changes begin in endolysosomal compartments.

### Propagation stage

Rab7⁺/LAMP1⁺ compartments repeatedly fuse.

### Pre-commitment stage

large vacuoles occupy increasing intracellular volume.

### Candidate commitment stage

vacuole fusion becomes sufficiently extensive to produce irreversible endolysosomal and whole-cell homeostatic failure.

### Terminal stage

plasma membrane becomes permeable and the cell dies.

No single biochemical transition corresponding to MOMP or MLKL activation has yet been identified.

---

# Final Mechanistic Interpretation

Floatptosis is best understood as an **emerging pathogen-controlled vacuolization-associated regulated cell-death process centered on pathological endolysosomal fusion**.

Its strongest currently supported pathway is:

**Bergeyella cardium variant or its OMVs/β-barrel proteins → intracellular delivery of bacterial cargo → unknown proximal host-sensing step → SLC9A9-dependent alteration of the late endosomal/lysosomal system → repeated fusion of Rab7⁺/LAMP1⁺ compartments → formation of giant vacuoles → displacement and compression of functional cytoplasm → disruption of vesicular and cellular homeostasis → secondary plasma-membrane permeabilization → death.**

The mechanistically distinctive feature is not vacuolization by itself.

It is the combination of:

**pathogen dependence + late endosome/lysosome origin + SLC9A9 requirement + pathological fusion.**

This separates floatptosis from methuosis, in which dysfunctional macropinosomes accumulate without normal lysosomal fusion, and from paraptosis, in which the dominant vacuoles originate largely from ER and mitochondrial swelling.

The principal core host protein currently identified is **SLC9A9/NHE9**, but SLC9A9 is not a terminal executioner.

It regulates the physicochemical environment and trafficking behavior of endolysosomal compartments.

The actual lethal process appears to arise from progressive fusion-driven destruction of intracellular compartmental organization.

The exact bridge from giant-vacuole overload to plasma-membrane permeabilization remains unresolved.

There is currently no demonstrated floatptosis-specific pore-forming protein, protease cascade, or lipid-peroxidative terminal mechanism.

Accordingly, the most defensible operational definition for the RCDome is:

**Floatptosis is an emerging pathogen-induced regulated cell-death modality, currently demonstrated principally in macrophages exposed to a virulent Bergeyella cardium variant or its outer-membrane components, in which SLC9A9-dependent pathological fusion of late endosomal/lysosomal compartments generates giant Rab7⁺/LAMP1⁺ vacuoles that progressively disrupt intracellular trafficking and homeostasis and culminate in secondary plasma-membrane permeabilization and death.**

For ontology purposes:

**Classification:** Emerging/candidate RCD modality

**Primary trigger:** *Bergeyella cardium* variant / OMVs / bacterial β-barrel-associated proteins

**Primary host cell evidence:** macrophages

**Core host dependency:** SLC9A9/NHE9

**Core structural lesion:** pathological fusion of late endosomal/lysosomal compartments

**Characteristic organelle markers:** Rab7⁺, LAMP1⁺

**Dedicated terminal executor:** not established

**Candidate point of no return:** extensive fused-vacuole burden causing irreversible endolysosomal/homeostatic collapse

**Terminal phenotype:** secondary plasma-membrane permeabilization and loss of cellular homeostasis

**Evidence status:** early-stage; not yet broadly replicated or canonical.
