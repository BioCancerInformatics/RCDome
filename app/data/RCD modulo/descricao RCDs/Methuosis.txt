Methuosis

1. Definition and Classification

Methuosis is an emerging non-apoptotic regulated cell-death phenotype characterized by excessive macropinocytosis and/or defective endosomal trafficking, progressive accumulation of large macropinosome-derived single-membrane vacuoles, disruption of intracellular organization and metabolism, and eventual loss of plasma-membrane integrity [1-5,30-31].

The defining lesion is not simply cytoplasmic vacuolization. Vacuolization occurs in numerous physiological and pathological processes and cannot by itself establish methuosis.

The defining feature is instead a causal trafficking disorder involving macropinosome formation, maturation, recycling, or delivery to functional lysosomes.

The canonical sequence is:

excessive macropinosome formation and/or defective macropinosome clearance → coalescence and enlargement of nonproductive single-membrane vacuoles → progressive cytoplasmic displacement and endolysosomal dysfunction → metabolic and structural deterioration → plasma-membrane rupture → cell death.

The vacuoles are generally derived from macropinosomes or related clathrin-independent endosomal compartments [1-5].

Methuosis vacuoles have a single membrane, whereas autophagosomes have double membranes surrounding luminal cytoplasmic contents [6].

Methuosis should also be distinguished from paraptosis. In methuosis, vacuoles originate predominantly from macropinosomal/endosomal compartments, whereas paraptotic vacuoles are principally associated with dilation of the endoplasmic reticulum and mitochondria [6-7].

It is similarly distinct from oncosis. Both can culminate in swelling and plasma-membrane failure, but methuosis begins with a specific disturbance in macropinocytosis/endosomal trafficking rather than with generalized primary ionic and osmotic collapse.

Its nomenclatural status remains less secure than that of apoptosis, necroptosis, ferroptosis, or pyroptosis. Methuosis is a unique form of cytolytic cell death that is somewhat selective for tumor cells and is distinct from apoptosis and other non-apoptotic cell death pathways such as autosis, paraptosis, and necroptosis [4].

For RCDome, methuosis is therefore most appropriately classified as an emerging regulated non-apoptotic vacuolar cell-death modality/phenotype with well-supported upstream trafficking biology but incompletely resolved terminal execution.

2. Initiation and Triggers

The classic initiation pathway is oncogenic Ras hyperactivation [28].

Constitutively active Ras can strongly stimulate macropinocytosis in glioblastoma cells, resulting in progressive vacuole accumulation and non-apoptotic death [2,8,26].

A major downstream component of this route is Rac1.

Rac1 controls actin remodeling required for plasma-membrane ruffling, formation of macropinocytic cups, and generation of macropinosomes [9-10].

Physiological macropinocytosis requires spatially and temporally regulated Rac1 activation followed by Rac1 deactivation during cup closure and vesicle maturation.

Persistent Rac1 activation disturbs this cycle.

Thus:

oncogenic Ras → persistent Rac1 activation → excessive membrane ruffling/macropinosome formation → abnormal vesicle influx.

In Ras-driven methuosis, Rac1 signaling is functionally connected to Arf6 suppression.

Activated Rac1 can interact with the Arf6 GTPase-activating protein GIT1, reducing the pool of active Arf6-GTP [8].

Arf6 normally supports recycling of clathrin-independent endosomal cargo toward the plasma membrane.

Therefore:

Ras → Rac1 activation → GIT1 engagement → reduced Arf6-GTP → impaired vesicle recycling.

This creates a particularly damaging combination:

increased macropinosome input + decreased macropinosome/endosome output.

A receptor-driven version of the pathway has been described in Ewing sarcoma.

CD99 engagement induces formation and internalization of an IGF-1R/RAS/Rac1-associated signaling complex, promoting excessive vacuolar trafficking and methuosis [11].

Thus:

CD99 engagement → IGF-1R/RAS/Rac1 signaling → abnormal macropinocytic/endocytic uptake → vacuole accumulation.

Methuosis can also be initiated without the canonical Ras/Rac1/Arf6 sequence.

Indole-based compounds such as MIPP and MOMIPP induce rapid formation of macropinocytic vesicles that acquire late-endosomal markers such as Rab7 and LAMP1 but fail to complete normal lysosomal trafficking [12-14].

PIKfyve has emerged as an important target in this chemically induced route [14-15].

PIKfyve is required for normal phosphoinositide regulation and endolysosomal membrane trafficking.

Its inhibition can produce extensive vacuolization and a methuosis-like death state.

Additional initiating routes include:

V-ATPase-dependent disruption of endolysosomal trafficking during maduramicin exposure [16]; [29].

VPS34-associated defects in endosome–lysosome fusion [17];

MKK4–p38α signaling during tubeimoside-2-associated methuosis [18];

and lipid-metabolic dysregulation involving HILPDA repression, decreased triacylglycerol storage, increased diacylglycerol, and stimulation of macropinosome formation [19].

Therefore, methuosis is not defined by one universal upstream receptor.

Its common initiating principle is excessive formation and/or defective clearance of macropinosome-derived compartments.

3. Core Mechanistic Machinery

Methuosis does not possess a single obligate death-signaling complex analogous to the apoptosome, necrosome, inflammasome, or mPTP.

Its best-supported core mechanism is a trafficking-failure state.

The central mechanistic imbalance can be expressed as:

macropinosome generation rate > recycling + maturation + lysosomal disposal capacity.

Several molecular systems contribute to this balance.

Rac1 controls actin-dependent generation of macropinosomes [9-10].

Arf6 supports recycling of clathrin-independent endosomal compartments [8].

Rab5 and Rab7 participate in early-to-late endosomal maturation [12-13].

PIKfyve controls phosphoinositide-dependent endolysosomal membrane identity and trafficking [14-15].

VPS34 supports endosomal maturation and endosome–lysosome fusion [17].

V-ATPase contributes to organelle acidification and, in some methuosis models, vesicular trafficking and vacuole formation [16,20].

The result of disruption is the formation of large, phase-lucent, single-membrane vacuoles.

These vacuoles may acquire some late-endosomal or lysosomal markers but frequently fail to become fully functional degradative lysosomes [12-13].

Consequently:

vesicles continue entering the pathway → recycling is impaired → maturation becomes incomplete → lysosomal delivery fails → vesicles fuse with one another → giant nonproductive vacuoles accumulate.

This trafficking state, rather than any single protein complex, constitutes the best-supported core machinery of methuosis.

4. Propagation and Amplification

The primary amplification mechanism is continued macropinosome production in the presence of defective disposal.

Each new macropinosome increases the membrane and fluid burden entering an already compromised trafficking network.

Fusion among macropinosomes and late endosomal compartments progressively generates larger vacuoles.

Thus:

persistent uptake → increasing vesicle number → vesicle fusion/coalescence → giant-vacuole accumulation.

Vacuoles can progressively occupy a large fraction of the cytoplasmic volume.

This displaces normal organelles and reduces the spatial organization required for physiological intracellular trafficking.

Endolysosomal dysfunction adds a second amplification mechanism.

Impaired maturation can interfere with receptor degradation, lysosomal protease processing, and cargo disposal [13].

Cathepsin maturation can therefore become abnormal without implying that cathepsins are universal methuosis executors.

Autophagic flux can also become compromised because functional autophagy ultimately depends on intact endolysosomal processing.

Thus, autophagic abnormalities occurring during methuosis may represent a downstream consequence of endolysosomal collapse rather than autophagy-dependent execution.

A third amplification mechanism involves metabolic failure.

Progressive vacuolization correlates with declining cellular metabolic activity in several methuosis models [11-13].

The mechanism likely reflects combined disruption of organelle organization, membrane trafficking, nutrient handling, receptor turnover, and energetic homeostasis.

A fourth late amplification mechanism involves cytoskeletal disruption.

Recent evidence indicates alterations in F-actin, tubulins, filamins, and RhoA–ROCK1-associated signaling during progression from extensive vacuolization toward terminal membrane damage [21].

This provides a mechanistic connection between intracellular vacuolar overload and loss of mechanical integrity.

However, the precise sequence linking vacuoles to cytoskeletal collapse remains incompletely defined.

5. Protective / Negative Regulators

The principal protective mechanisms are the physiological processes that prevent macropinosome accumulation.

Arf6-GTP supports recycling of clathrin-independent endosomes and macropinosomal membranes toward the plasma membrane [8].

In Ras-driven methuosis, reduction of Arf6-GTP contributes directly to accumulation of vacuoles.

Therefore, intact Arf6-dependent recycling is an important protective mechanism.

Normal Rac1 cycling is also protective.

Macropinocytosis requires Rac1 activation, but continued Rac1 activation without appropriate deactivation can become pathological [9-10].

Thus, the protective state is not simply “low Rac1,” but tightly regulated spatial and temporal Rac1 activity.

Rab5/Rab7-dependent trafficking protects by supporting orderly progression through endosomal compartments [12-13].

PIKfyve protects by maintaining phosphoinositide-dependent endolysosomal identity and trafficking [14-15].

VPS34 similarly contributes to endosome maturation and fusion with lysosomes [17].

Loss or inhibition of these trafficking systems can be sufficient to create a methuotic state rather than merely sensitizing the cell.

V-ATPase has a more context-dependent role.

In several models, inhibiting V-ATPase reduces vacuolization or death, implying that V-ATPase activity contributes to formation or maintenance of the pathological vacuolar compartment [16,20].

However, other methuosis-like systems contain acidic vacuoles and can respond differently to V-ATPase inhibition [22].

Therefore, V-ATPase should not be assigned a universal protective or pro-death role.

ESCRT-III represents a late protective system.

CHMP3, CHMP5, and related ESCRT components can limit membrane damage produced during advanced methuosis [21].

ESCRT-mediated repair therefore appears to act downstream of vacuole formation by delaying catastrophic plasma-membrane failure.

6. Commitment / Point of No Return

No universal molecular point of no return has been identified for methuosis.

Importantly, vacuolization itself is not necessarily irreversible.

Early vacuolization can regress in some experimental models after removal of the initiating insult or restoration of normal trafficking [16-17].

Therefore:

vacuolization ≠ commitment to death.

Before commitment, rescue can occur by reducing macropinosome formation or restoring endosomal trafficking.

Examples include inhibition of Rac1-dependent macropinocytosis, pharmacological suppression of macropinocytic uptake, restoration of VPS34-associated trafficking, or interference with V-ATPase-dependent vacuolar processes in responsive models [8,16-17,23].

The most defensible commitment model is therefore threshold-based.

Commitment likely occurs when vacuole burden and trafficking failure become sufficiently extensive that cellular architecture, metabolic activity, lysosomal function, cytoskeletal integrity, and membrane-repair capacity can no longer recover.

A conceptual sequence is:

reversible macropinosome accumulation → progressive trafficking collapse → giant vacuolar occupation of the cytoplasm → metabolic decline + cytoskeletal destabilization + membrane stress → irreversible homeostatic failure.

No individual protein has yet been established as the universal molecular switch separating reversible vacuolization from lethal methuosis.

7. Execution and Terminal Cause of Death

What physically kills the cell in methuosis?

The available evidence does not support a dedicated methuosis-specific protease, pore-forming protein, or enzymatic executor.

The best-supported execution model is progressive structural, metabolic, and biomechanical failure caused by uncontrolled vacuole accumulation.

As vacuoles enlarge and occupy increasing cytoplasmic volume, they displace normal organelles, disrupt membrane trafficking, interfere with receptor and cargo processing, compromise endolysosomal function, and are associated with declining metabolic capacity [11-13].

Activated Ras can induce non-apoptotic cell death involving Rac1–GIT1–Arf6 and endosomal trafficking [8].

The final observable lethal event is loss of plasma-membrane integrity.

Thus, the best-supported physical sequence is:

macropinosome/endosomal overload → giant vacuole accumulation → intracellular architecture disruption + metabolic decline + cytoskeletal destabilization → membrane stress exceeding repair capacity → catastrophic plasma-membrane rupture → irreversible ionic and metabolic collapse.

Importantly, the terminal membrane rupture appears independent of MLKL and GSDMD in the models described [21].

Therefore, methuosis does not use the established pore-forming executors of necroptosis or pyroptosis.

Whether NINJ1 contributes to terminal plasma-membrane rupture remains unresolved.

NINJ1 is an established general mediator of terminal membrane rupture in several lytic death pathways [24-25], but the supplied literature does not provide direct evidence establishing NINJ1 as the terminal methuosis executor.

Accordingly:

NINJ1 = plausible candidate for terminal rupture;

not:

NINJ1 = established methuosis executor.

The strongest current answer to “what physically kills the cell?” is therefore catastrophic plasma-membrane failure following vacuole-driven biomechanical and homeostatic collapse.

8. Morphological Sequence

Methuosis has a characteristic chronological morphology.

Early stages begin with pronounced plasma-membrane ruffling and increased fluid-phase uptake.

Small macropinosomes form beneath the plasma membrane.

These vesicles subsequently enlarge and fuse.

The morphological progression can be represented as:

membrane ruffling → small macropinosomes → macropinosome fusion/coalescence → large phase-lucent single-membrane vacuoles → extensive cytoplasmic occupation → displacement/compression of organelles → cell rounding and structural deterioration → plasma-membrane rupture.

The vacuoles typically do not contain cytoplasmic organelles, distinguishing them from canonical autophagosomes [1-3,8].

In many models they acquire markers associated with late endosomes, including Rab7 or LAMP1, but fail to complete normal lysosomal maturation [12-13].

The nucleus typically lacks the classical apoptotic sequence of chromatin condensation, nuclear fragmentation, and apoptotic-body formation [1-3].

Cell shrinkage is also not the dominant terminal morphology.

Instead, death ultimately resembles lytic or necrosis-like structural failure.

However, large cytoplasmic vacuoles alone are insufficient for diagnosis because paraptosis, autophagic abnormalities, lysosomal swelling, ER stress, and several toxic insults can produce superficially similar morphology.

Vacuole origin must therefore be experimentally established.

9. Crosstalk and Differential Diagnosis

The most important differential diagnosis is apoptosis.

Methuosis is typically resistant to broad caspase inhibition and lacks canonical apoptotic nuclear fragmentation [2-3,8].

Therefore, caspase activation observed in a particular model should not automatically be interpreted as the core methuosis executor.

Some experimental systems can show simultaneous apoptosis and methuosis, representing pathway coexistence rather than evidence that methuosis is an apoptotic subtype.

Autophagy must also be distinguished carefully.

Methuosis vacuoles have a single membrane, whereas autophagosomes have double membranes surrounding luminal cytoplasmic contents [6].

ATG5 deficiency does not abolish methuotic death in relevant experimental models [6].

Autophagic flux can become impaired because endolysosomal trafficking is disrupted, but this is not equivalent to autophagy-dependent cell death.

Paraptosis is another important differential diagnosis.

Both produce extensive cytoplasmic vacuolization.

However:

methuosis → macropinosome/endosome-derived vacuoles;

paraptosis → primarily ER/mitochondria-derived vacuoles [7].

Necroptosis is distinguished by RIPK1/RIPK3/MLKL signaling.

Necrostatin-1 fails to rescue CD99-induced methuosis, and terminal methuosis-associated membrane damage does not require MLKL in the supplied evidence [11,21].

Pyroptosis is distinguished by gasdermin-mediated plasma-membrane pores.

Methuosis-associated terminal lysis appears GSDMD-independent [21].

Oncosis overlaps morphologically because both may display cellular swelling and membrane failure.

However, methuosis has a specific upstream macropinocytic/endosomal trafficking lesion, whereas oncosis primarily describes severe ionic/osmotic failure and swelling.

Lysosomal cell death can overlap with methuosis because defective endolysosomal trafficking may disturb lysosomal protease maturation or lysosome function.

However, the supplied evidence does not establish primary lysosomal membrane permeabilization and cathepsin execution as universal methuosis mechanisms.

10. Experimental Validation

A convincing demonstration of methuosis requires showing that the vacuoles originate from macropinocytosis/endosomal trafficking rather than from another organelle.

Live-cell microscopy is particularly important because it can establish the temporal sequence of membrane ruffling, vesicle formation, vacuole fusion, cytoplasmic occupation, and terminal membrane failure.

Fluid-phase tracers such as fluorescent dextran or Lucifer yellow can demonstrate macropinocytic uptake [2,8,27].

Strong evidence therefore includes:

increased fluid-phase uptake → tracer accumulation within vacuoles → progressive vacuole enlargement.

Ultrastructural analysis should demonstrate single-membrane vacuoles rather than classical double-membrane autophagosomes.

Endosomal markers should be examined.

Rab5, Rab7, LAMP1, Arf6, or related trafficking markers can identify the maturation state and origin of the vacuolar compartment [12-13].

The key is not merely marker positivity but demonstration of abnormal progression through the endosomal system.

Lysosomal fusion and acidification should also be tested.

Failure of mature lysosomal fusion or degradation supports the nonproductive trafficking model.

Causal rescue is essential.

Depending on the specific model, this can include perturbation of Rac1, macropinocytosis, PIKfyve, VPS34, V-ATPase, or the identified initiating trigger [8,14-17,23].

A particularly strong causal chain is:

trigger → increased macropinocytosis/trafficking defect → macropinosome-derived vacuolization → intervention blocking the identified upstream mechanism → reduced vacuolization + rescued viability.

Alternative death pathways should be excluded experimentally.

Relevant approaches include caspase inhibition, assessment of BAX/BAK dependence, RIPK1/RIPK3/MLKL perturbation, gasdermin analysis, and autophagy-gene perturbation where appropriate.

Plasma-membrane failure should be measured independently using permeability assays rather than inferred solely from morphology.

Transcriptomics alone cannot establish active methuosis.

Expression of RAS, RAC1, ARF6, RAB5, RAB7, PIKFYVE, VPS34, V-ATPase components, LAMP genes, or macropinocytosis-associated genes may indicate a methuosis-associated trafficking state or susceptibility phenotype.

It cannot prove excessive macropinocytosis, macropinosome-derived vacuole accumulation, or active methuotic execution.

11. Molecular Hierarchy

Initiators / triggers:

oncogenic Ras;

CD99–IGF-1R signaling;

methuosis-inducing indolyl-pyridinyl-propenones;

PIKfyve inhibition;

VPS34 inhibition;

maduramicin;

MKK4–p38α-associated signaling;

HILPDA repression and DAG accumulation;

other context-specific pharmacological or metabolic insults.

Early transducers:

Ras;

Rac1;

GIT1;

Arf6;

actin-remodeling machinery;

IGF-1R in receptor-driven Ewing sarcoma models.

Core trafficking machinery:

macropinosome formation;

Arf6-dependent recycling;

Rab5/Rab7-dependent endosomal maturation;

PIKfyve-dependent endolysosomal trafficking;

VPS34-dependent fusion/maturation;

context-dependent V-ATPase activity.

Core pathological state:

continued macropinosome input + defective recycling/maturation/lysosomal clearance.

Execution-linked changes:

vacuole coalescence;

giant single-membrane vacuole accumulation;

cytoplasmic displacement;

defective cargo degradation;

abnormal cathepsin maturation;

autophagic-flux impairment;

metabolic decline;

cytoskeletal disruption;

plasma-membrane stress.

Protective mechanisms:

physiological Rac1 cycling;

Arf6-GTP-dependent recycling;

normal Rab5/Rab7 maturation;

PIKfyve function;

VPS34 function;

functional endosome–lysosome trafficking;

ESCRT-III-mediated membrane repair.

Established universal terminal executor:

none.

Candidate late lysis factor:

NINJ1, currently unproven specifically for methuosis.

Terminal lesion:

catastrophic loss of plasma-membrane integrity following vacuole-driven structural and homeostatic collapse.

12. Integrated Canonical Sequence

Canonical Ras route:

oncogenic Ras activation → persistent Rac1 signaling → extensive actin-driven membrane ruffling → hyperstimulation of macropinocytosis → Rac1/GIT1-associated reduction of Arf6-GTP → impaired vesicle recycling → macropinosome accumulation → vesicle coalescence → giant vacuoles → progressive trafficking and metabolic dysfunction → cytoskeletal destabilization → membrane stress → terminal rupture.

Receptor-driven route:

CD99 engagement → IGF-1R/RAS/Rac1 complex formation → internalization/macropinocytic trafficking → defective vacuole processing → progressive vacuolization → methuotic death.

PIKfyve/trafficking route:

MIPP/MOMIPP or direct PIKfyve inhibition → abnormal Rab5/Rab7-dependent endosomal progression → late-endosomal vacuoles that fail productive lysosomal fusion → giant vacuolar accumulation → homeostatic failure → terminal rupture.

VPS34 route:

VPS34 dysfunction → defective endosome–lysosome fusion → reversible early vacuolization → persistent trafficking failure → extensive vacuole accumulation → death.

V-ATPase-associated route:

maduramicin-associated trafficking disturbance → altered Rab11/Arf6/Rab7/LAMP1 state + V-ATPase V0-dependent endolysosomal block → extensive vacuolization → structural and metabolic deterioration → terminal death.

Integrated pathway:

triggering lesion → excessive macropinosome formation and/or defective endosomal maturation → failure of Arf6-, Rab5/Rab7-, PIKfyve-, VPS34-, or lysosome-directed trafficking → fusion and expansion of nonproductive single-membrane vacuoles → displacement of cytoplasm and organelles → defective receptor/cargo degradation + impaired lysosomal/autophagic processing → declining metabolic capacity → cytoskeletal destabilization → vacuolar and membrane stress exceed repair capacity → plasma-membrane rupture → irreversible ionic/homeostatic collapse → death.

13. Final Mechanistic Interpretation

For RCDome, methuosis should be classified as an emerging but mechanistically recognizable non-apoptotic vacuolar regulated cell-death phenotype centered on dysregulated macropinocytosis and endosomal trafficking failure.

It is more than simple vacuolization.

Its defining causal architecture is:

excessive macropinosome production and/or failed macropinosome clearance → accumulation of large macropinosome-derived single-membrane vacuoles → intracellular trafficking collapse → structural and metabolic deterioration → terminal lysis.

The strongest canonical signaling model is:

Ras → Rac1 activation → Arf6 suppression → excessive macropinocytosis + defective recycling.

However, this pathway is not universal.

PIKfyve inhibition, VPS34 dysfunction, V-ATPase-associated trafficking abnormalities, CD99–IGF-1R signaling, MKK4–p38α signaling, and metabolic alterations can converge on a similar methuotic phenotype.

Therefore, Ras and Rac1 should not be defined as obligatory universal methuosis components.

Likewise, vacuolization alone should never be considered sufficient evidence.

The macropinosomal/endosomal origin of the vacuoles and a causal trafficking defect must be established.

There is currently no universally established methuosis-specific executor.

MLKL and GSDMD are not required in the best-characterized terminal membrane-damage models, separating methuosis from canonical necroptosis and pyroptosis.

Cathepsins, mitochondrial dysfunction, ER stress, lysosomal permeabilization, and autophagy abnormalities may participate in particular contexts, but none is established as the universal terminal execution machinery.

NINJ1 is a plausible convergent lytic factor but remains unproven specifically in methuosis.

The most defensible terminal cause of death is therefore catastrophic plasma-membrane rupture resulting from progressive vacuole-driven structural, biomechanical, metabolic, and homeostatic failure.

The main unresolved question is consequently very specific:

how does extensive intracellular vacuole accumulation become converted into the final plasma-membrane rupture?

Until this step is molecularly resolved, RCDome should distinguish clearly between the established upstream methuotic state and the unresolved terminal lytic machinery.

For transcriptomic applications, signatures involving macropinocytosis, RAC1, ARF6, RAB proteins, PIKFYVE, VPS34, V-ATPase components, lysosomal trafficking, or cytoskeletal genes should be described as methuosis-associated or macropinocytosis/trafficking-associated states.

They cannot demonstrate active methuosis without direct evidence of macropinosome-derived vacuolization, trafficking collapse, and cell-death execution.
